## 1. Which tariffs and trade measures apply to imported building materials (China, Vietnam, Canada, Mexico) as of Sept 25, 2026?

### Takeaway
Rates have been rebuilt almost from scratch since February 2026:
- **Gone:**
  - The **IEEPA "reciprocal" and "fentanyl" tariffs**, struck down by the Supreme Court on **Feb 20, 2026**. Refunds are in progress, partly held up by litigation.
  - The **Section 122 bridge tariff**, in force **Feb 24 – Jul 24, 2026**.
- **Now in force:**
  - **Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs of 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies** (from **Jul 24, 2026**). China and Vietnam pay 12.5%. USMCA-qualifying goods and all Section 232 goods are exempt.
  - **Legacy Section 301 tariffs on China.**
  - **Section 232** on wood products: 10% on softwood lumber; 25% on kitchen cabinets and vanities, **rising to 50% on Jan 1, 2027**.
  - **Section 232 on steel, aluminum and copper**, restructured from **Apr 6, 2026**: 50%, 25% or 15% tiers on full customs value.
  - A new **Section 338 tariff of 50% on ~554 Canadian tariff lines**, including plywood, MDF and hardwood lumber (from **Aug 22, 2026**). Cement and switchgear were removed **Sept 15**.
  - A **Section 201 safeguard on quartz surface products**: 25% in-quota / 50% over-quota from **Aug 15, 2026**.
  - Deep **AD/CVD orders** on Chinese cabinets, tile, quartz and plywood. New plywood orders cover China, Indonesia and Vietnam.
- **Truce:** The US–China truce was **extended on Sept 23–24, 2026 to Jan 10, 2027**. A pending "excess capacity" Section 301 action, reportedly recommending **7.5% on Chinese goods**, was held back until after the Xi summit.

### Cited Findings

**1a. IEEPA tariffs: struck down, refunds in progress**
- The Supreme Court held on **Feb 20, 2026** that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs imposed under it. The case is Learning Resources, consolidated with V.O.S. Selections; one summary captions it "Learning Resources v. United States". Sources: [Penn Wharton Budget Model, 2026-02-20](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2026-02-20-supreme-court-tariff-ruling/); [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/blog/the-supreme-courts-ieepa-tariff-ruling-next-steps-potential-refunds-and/); [Honigman alert (summary)](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462).
- **CIT refund order.** The Court of International Trade (Judge Richard Eaton) ordered refunds of IEEPA duties to all importers of record but did not mandate a single process. The government appealed the order. Source: [Holland & Knight, June 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals) (summary).
- **Federal Circuit.** A later ruling "clears the way for IEEPA tariff refund litigation to resume." Source: [Buchalter](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/one-small-step-for-importers-federal-circuit-clears-the-way-for-ieepa-tariff-refund-litigation-to-resume/) (date not captured).
- **CAPE (CBP's refund process).**
  - Phase 1 opened **Apr 20, 2026**. It covers certain unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within 80 days of the CAPE submission.
  - Phase 2 opened **Jun 29, 2026**. It adds entries flagged for reconciliation.
  - Entries outside the CAPE windows need a protest within **180 days of liquidation** (19 U.S.C. 1514).
  - Refunds are typically paid 60–90 days after a CAPE declaration is accepted.
  - Sources: [Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/06/cbp-confirms-june-29-2026-ieepa-tariff-refund-process-phase-2-launch/); [White and Williams](https://www.whiteandwilliams.com/resources-alerts-IEEPA-Tariff-Refunds-CBP-Launches-CAPE-Process); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/44450c73/cbp-issues-tariff-refund-instructions); [BDO FAQ](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/ieepa-tariff-refunds-frequently-asked-questions) (summary). **Conflict:** one summary says refund requests are due "within 90 days" of liquidation. Treat CBP's CSMS guidance as authoritative.
- **Refund scale.** CBP can refund electronically about **82% of entries** that paid IEEPA duties: ~**8.3 million entries** worth ~**$35.46B** including interest, as of mid-2026. Sources: summary drawing on [H&K](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals) and [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/); the exact originating page was not confirmed.
- **Still delayed.** As of **Sept 21–22, 2026**, "some refunds remain caught between Customs and the courts." Source: [Stacker via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/stacker-international/2026/09/22/months-after-ieepa-tariffs-were-struck-down-some-refunds-remain-caught-between-customs-and-the-courts/).

**1b. Section 122 bridge tariff: expired**
- **Timeline.** Proclaimed right after the ruling; effective **Feb 24, 2026** for 150 days; expired **12:01 a.m. Jul 24, 2026**. Sources: [Honigman (summary)](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462); [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/reports/S122-US-Tariff-Estimates).
- **Rate conflict.**
  - Global Trade Alert says the surcharge was **raised from 10% to 15% on Feb 22**.
  - Law firms describe a **10%** tariff through expiry: [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/trump-administration-imposes-10-section-122-tariff-plan-replace-ieepa-tariffs), [Ward and Smith on the CIT case](https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/court-of-international-trade-rejects-10-section-122-tariff-what-businesses-should-know-while-the-appeal-proceeds), [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462).
  - The law-firm and court naming ("10%") looks more authoritative for the rate actually collected. Verify in the proclamation or CSMS.
- **Exclusion.** Goods already subject to Section 232 (steel, aluminum, copper, lumber, autos) were excluded to the extent the 232 tariff applied. Source: [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/reports/S122-US-Tariff-Estimates) (summary).
- **Litigation.** The CIT struck down the Section 122 tariffs in **May 2026**. The ruling is on appeal, and its practical impact is "limited." Source: [Skadden, May 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs).

**1c. Section 301: legacy China lists, new "forced-labor" tariffs, pending "excess capacity" case**
- **Forced-labor tariffs: proposal.** USTR proposed them on **Jun 2, 2026**: 10% on most goods of 15 partners and 12.5% on 45 partners. Final action took effect **12:01 a.m. Jul 24, 2026**, covering **60 economies**. Sources: [USTR press release](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations); [White House action](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/07/actions-by-the-united-states-in-the-investigations-under-section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974-of-the-acts-policies-and-practices-of-60-economies-related-to-the-failure-of-each-economy-to-impose-and/); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/and-the-tariff-beat-goes-on); [C.H. Robinson, 2026-07-24](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/07-24-26-client-advisory-new-section-301-forced-labor-tariffs-now-in-effect/).
- **Forced-labor tariffs: rates by country.**
  - **10%** applies to economies that have a forced-labor import ban but do not enforce it effectively. This includes **Canada, Mexico and the EU**.
  - **12.5%** applies to all others, including **Vietnam** and **China**.
  - For China, summaries say the combined Section 301 burden becomes **37.5%** (12.5% plus the legacy 25%).
  - Sources: [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/forced-labor-meet-section-301-new-tariffs-target-60-of-americas-biggest-trading-partners/); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/ustr-section-301-forced-labor-60-economies/); [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action) (summary).
- **Forced-labor tariffs: exemptions.**
  - Goods entered duty-free under **USMCA** are exempt, as are CAFTA-DR textile and apparel goods.
  - **All articles subject to Section 232 are exempt.** That covers steel, aluminum, copper and their derivatives; autos and parts; medium and heavy trucks; **wood products**; and semiconductors.
  - Sources: [USTR fact sheet](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor); [CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887); [IFF USA importer guide](https://www.iffusa.com/post/section-301-forced-labor-tariff-importer-guide) (summary).
- **CBP filing order.** The forced-labor Chapter 99 code is reported **first**, ahead of Section 122, 232 and 201 codes. This implies it can stack with a Section 201 safeguard. Source: [CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887) (summary).
- **Excess-capacity case: scope.** USTR opened Section 301 investigations on **Mar 11, 2026** into "structural excess capacity" in **16 economies**: China, EU, Singapore, Switzerland, Norway, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Mexico, Japan and India. Sources: [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-16-us-trade-partners-targeting-industrial); [Federal Register, 2026-03-17](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/17/2026-05214/initiation-of-section-301-investigations-acts-policies-and-practices-of-certain-economies-relating).
- **Excess-capacity case: schedule.** Comments were due **Apr 15** and hearings ran **May 5–8, 2026**. The unofficial target for remedies was **Jul 24, 2026**. Sources: [USTR hearings notice](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/may/public-hearings-regarding-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/answering-the-top-seven-questions-about-pending-section-301-comment-deadlines).
- **Excess-capacity case: status.** No determination or remedy had been published by late September 2026. The statutory window runs to about **March 2027**. Source: [ICPA Section 301 tracker](https://www.icpainc.org/section-301-tracker/) (summary).
- **Bloomberg, Sept 17, 2026.** The US was expected to hold off announcing new tariffs until after the Xi–Trump summit. The pending excess-capacity report would recommend a **7.5% tariff on Chinese goods**, and the final rate was uncertain. Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/us-said-to-delay-excess-capacity-tariffs-until-after-xi-summit); [TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/us-delays-tariffs-plan-china); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10877969).
- **Legacy China tariff on ceramic tile.** USTR imposed a **10% Section 301 duty on ceramic tile from Sept 24, 2018**, later raised to **25%**. Source: [USITC Ceramic Tile from China review report](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/701_731/pub5687.pdf) (summary).

**1d. US–China arrangement**
- **Nov 1, 2025 deal (Busan).** The US cut the fentanyl-related IEEPA tariff by 10 points effective **Nov 10, 2025** and kept the "heightened reciprocal" tariffs suspended **until Nov 10, 2026**. Sources: [White House fact sheet, Nov 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-United-States-and-China-Negotiate-One-Year-Trade-Deal).
- **Section 301 exclusions.** USTR extended **178 exclusions** from Nov 29, 2025 to **Nov 10, 2026**. Source: [USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation).
- **Truce extension.** On **Sept 23–24, 2026**, Treasury Secretary Bessent said the truce is **extended two months, from Nov 10, 2026 to Jan 10, 2027**. The announcement followed an unscheduled meeting with Vice Premier He Lifeng, as Xi began a state visit to Washington. Sources: [CNBC, 2026-09-24](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/us-china-trade-truce-bessent-trump-xi.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/us-china-extend-trade-truce-trump-rcna599525); [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/us-china-to-extend-trade-war-truce-by-2-months/831251/); [Customs & International Trade Law blog, 2026-09-25](https://customsandinternationaltradelaw.com/2026/09/25/breaking-trade-news-coac-meeting-recommendations-u-s-china-trade-truce-extended/).
- **"Board of Trade" talks.** The two sides may agree lists of goods for lower tariffs under a "Board of Trade" initiative: consumer and low-tech goods from China; energy, agricultural goods and medical devices from the US. Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/us-china-trade-truce-bessent-trump-xi.html) (summary).

**1e. Section 232: wood products**
- **Sept 29, 2025 proclamation**, effective **Oct 14, 2025**:
  - **10%** on softwood timber and lumber.
  - **25%** on certain upholstered wooden products, **kitchen cabinets and vanities**.
  - Planned step-up for cabinets and vanities to **50% on Jan 1, 2026**.
  - Sources: [Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/01/president-trump-delays-section-232-tariff-increase-on-wood-furniture-cabinets-and-vanities/); [Sandler Travis](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-232-tariff-timber-lumber); [RVIA](https://www.rvia.org/news-insights/section-232-tariffs-wood-products-announced).
- **Increase delayed.** A proclamation on **Dec 31, 2025** delayed the increase by one year, **to Jan 1, 2027**, citing "productive negotiations." Rates stay at **25%** through 2026. Sources: [Barnes Richardson](https://www.barnesrichardson.com/upholstered-wooden-products-kitchen-cabinets-and-vanities-tariff-hike-delayed); [Mohawk Global](https://mohawkglobal.com/word-from-the-white-house/furniture-and-cabinet-imports-section-232-tariff-increase-delayed-to-2027/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=29f94cee-4db1-40ac-9220-4321e9e47b96); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/us-tariffs-lumber-upholstered-furniture-cabinets-vanities/).
- **Canadian lumber total.** With the 232 lumber tariff, total duties on Canadian softwood lumber reached about **45%**: AD/CVD of about 35% plus 232 at 10%. Sources: [NAHB, Sept 2025](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2025/09/section-232-tariffs); [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/canadian-lumber-tariffs-rise-nearly-40-nahb-reports) (summary).

**1f. Section 232: steel, aluminum, copper (restructured 2026)**
- **Apr 2, 2026 proclamation**, effective **Apr 6, 2026**:
  - Duties now apply to the **full customs value**, not just metal content.
  - **50%** on products made entirely or almost entirely of steel, aluminum or copper.
  - **25%** on derivatives with **>15%** metal content.
  - Derivatives with **<15%** metal content are no longer tariffed.
  - **10%** if made with US-origin metal.
  - A **15% transitional rate** for metal-intensive industrial equipment and **electrical grid equipment through Dec 31, 2027**.
  - Sources: [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/us-adjusts-section-232-tariffs-on-aluminum-steel-and-copper-full-customs-value-now-applies/); [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/blog/section-232-changes-for-aluminum-copper-steel/); [Phillips Lytle](https://phillipslytle.com/administration-restructures-section-232-tariffs-on-metal-and-derivative-products/); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/section-232-metals-tariffs-expanded-and-recalibrated-what-importers-need-to-know) (summary).
- **June 2026 changes.** More products became eligible for the lower rates through 2027. Tariffs were lowered on certain imports from specific partners. The US-metal content threshold dropped from 95% to 85%. Several derivatives were added. Same sources (summary).
- **No expiry.** 232 metals rates "remain at a 50% rate with no expiration." Source: [ABC Carolinas](https://abccarolinas.org/construction-material-costs-2026-2027-tariff-transition-risks-and-actions-for-carolinas-contractors/) (summary).

**1g. Section 338: new 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, including wood panels**
- **Announcement.** On **Jul 20, 2026** the President invoked **Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930** to impose **50%** additional tariffs on certain Canadian goods, **including USMCA-compliant goods**. The stated reason is "unequal treatment" of US commerce. Sources: [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-President-Trump-Imposes-New-50-Tariffs-on-Certain-Canadian-Imports); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/50-percent-opening-bid-canadian-imports-subject-to-section-338-tariffs); [White House fact sheet, Jul 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/07/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-imposes-additional-tariffs-on-canada/).
- **Effective date.** Originally set for Aug 19. After a **three-day suspension**, it took effect **12:01 a.m. Aug 22, 2026**. Sources: [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/three-day-suspension-of-section-338-additional-duties-on-canadian-imports/); [C.H. Robinson, 2026-08-22](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/08-22-26-client-advisory-new-50-percent-section-338-duties-on-select-canadian-products/).
- **Coverage.**
  - About **554 HTS codes**, roughly **$20B** of annual imports.
  - Wood products covered include hardwood lumber, fiberboard, **MDF**, **plywood** (nearly all of **HTS 4412**), laminated lumber and veneered panels.
  - **Softwood lumber and timber are excluded** because they fall under Section 232.
  - Sources: [Gateway Lines](https://gatewaylines.com/press-releases/section-338-canada-50-percent-covered-products) (summary); [Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/elbows-back-up-u-s-section-338-tariffs-canada-retaliatory-surtaxes-current-landscape/), via Perplexity; [BC Wood report](https://bcwood.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Canada_WoodTarrifs_Report.pdf); [The Architect's Newspaper, Aug 2026](https://www.archpaper.com/2026/08/canada-plywood-tariffs/); [KCMA](https://kcma.org/insights/president-trumps-new-section-338-tariffs-products-canada).
- **Rate conflict.** Most sources say a flat **50%**. A Perplexity answer citing Osler says "**25% to 50%** depending on the good." The law-firm headlines (Wiley, H&K) say 50%; check the Annex rates.
- **Stacking.** Section 338 duties apply on top of other duties such as Section 301. Sources: [Cement Institute](https://thecementinstitute.com/u-s-to-hit-canada-with-50-tariff-on-cement/); [NPCA](https://precast.org/blog/npcas-advocacy-against-canadian-cement-tariffs/) (summary).
- **Sept 15, 2026 list change.** A proclamation signed **Sept 8** (Federal Register Sept 14) modified the list for goods entered from **Sept 15, 2026**.
  - **Removed:** ordinary Portland cement (HTS 2523.29.00), **switchgear assemblies and switchboards**, rock salt, sugar and others.
  - **Added:** some steel and aluminum items, metal fittings and welding inputs, furniture and lamps, and others.
  - Sources: [CemNet](https://www.cemnet.com/News/story/182089/us-removes-50-tariff-on-canadian-cement.html); [Global Cement](https://www.globalcement.com/news/21248-us-removes-tariffs-upon-cement-imported-from-canada); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/tariffs-removed-added-9.7343778).
- **Canada's response.** Canada's counter-tariffs on US goods took effect **Sept 8, 2026**. Sources: [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/08/list-of-products-from-the-united-states-subject-to-counter-tariffs-effective-september-8-2026.html); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/carney-says-new-canadian-tariffs-us-goods-will-come-into-effect-september-8-2026-08-22/).
- **Unverified Sept 29 changes.** One low-authority site describes further Section 338 changes on Sept 29, 2026: [tariffcalculator2026.com](https://tariffcalculator2026.com/canada-section-338-september-15-29-what-changes). No authoritative source confirmed this.

**1h. Section 201 safeguard on quartz surface products (countertops)**
- **Proclamation.** After an affirmative ITC injury finding, a **Jul 31, 2026** proclamation imposed a **four-year tariff-rate quota**.
  - Effective **12:01 a.m. Aug 15, 2026**, through **Aug 14, 2030**.
  - Additional duty is **25% within** the quarterly quota and **50% above** it.
  - Quota quarters run Aug 15–Nov 14, Nov 15–Feb 13, Feb 14–May 15 and May 16–Aug 14.
  - Sources: [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/07/to-facilitate-positive-adjustment-to-competition-from-imports-of-quartz-surface-products/); [Diaz Trade Law](https://diaztradelaw.com/new-section-201-safeguard-tariffs-on-quartz-surface-products/); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/quartz-surface-products-safeguard-tariff-rate-quota); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/08/united-states-quartz-surface-products-safeguard-trq.html); [KCMA](https://kcma.org/insights/quartz-surface-products-201-safeguard-determination).
- **Exempt countries.** Canada, Mexico, Australia, CAFTA-DR countries, Colombia, **Israel**, **Korea**, Panama, Peru, Singapore and CBERA beneficiaries. Also exempt: developing countries in Note 41(c) that stay under the import-share thresholds (<3% each, <9% combined). Sources: [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/quartz-surface-products-safeguard-tariff-rate-quota); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/08/united-states-quartz-surface-products-safeguard-trq.html) (summary).
- **Vietnam is covered.** Source: [VCCI WTO Center (Vietnam)](https://wtocenter.vn/tin-tuc/31096-us-establishes-4-year-tariff-rate-quota-safeguard-on-quartz-surface-imports-from-vietnam).
- **Tariff codes.** Covered HTS: **6810.99.0020, 6810.99.0040, 7020.00.6000**. Chapter 99 codes: **9903.45.30** (in-quota, 25%) and **9903.45.31** (over-quota, 50%). Source: Perplexity answer citing [GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/briefings/new-4-year-safeguard-trq-with-up-to-50-e78f0e70) and the [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/07/to-facilitate-positive-adjustment-to-competition-from-imports-of-quartz-surface-products/).

**1i. AD/CVD orders relevant to building materials**
- **Wooden cabinets and vanities (China).**
  - Orders A-570-106 (AD) and C-570-107 (CVD), effective **Apr 21, 2020**, continued **Sept 17, 2025** after the ITC sunset review.
  - Investigation-stage rates: **AD 4.37%–262.18%** (China-wide **262.18%**); **CVD 13.33%–293.45%**.
  - Scope: assembled, unassembled and RTA ("flat pack") cabinets and vanities under HTS 9403.40.9060 and 9403.60.8081, with components under 9403.90.7080.
  - Sources: [GingerControl](https://gingercontrol.com/industries/furniture-home) (summary, low-authority); [Commerce fact sheet, Feb 24, 2020](https://enforcement.trade.gov/download/factsheets/factsheet-prc-wooden-cabinets-vanities-ad-cvd-final-022420.pdf); [USITC sunset, Aug 21, 2025](https://www.usitc.gov/press_room/news_release/2025/er0821_67441.htm).
- **Cabinets: 2026 reviews.**

## 4. How are tariffs changing the cost of a home, apartment unit or commercial project, and how are builders responding?

### Takeaway
- **Cost per home:** the most-cited figures run **$7,500–$10,900 per single-family home** (NAHB 2025 surveys; JEC April 2026). Brookings estimates about **$30B** added across residential construction.
- **Commercial projects:** tariffs are estimated to add about **3% to total project cost**.
- **Multifamily:** no credible per-unit estimate was found. This is a gap Quotr could fill.
- **What builders are doing:**
  - Rewriting escalation clauses to name tariffs specifically, with index-based, two-way adjustments.
  - Buying ahead and locking prices at bid.
  - Shortening quote validity.
  - Shifting sourcing (Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, or exempt origins for quartz) while facing circumvention rules.
  - Lobbying for a housing tariff exclusion process (H.R. 10416).

### Inferences
- **Per-home estimates are dated.** The widely cited figures come from 2025 surveys and a report that excludes lumber. None reflects the post-February 2026 regime (IEEPA gone; forced-labor 301, Section 338, the quartz safeguard and the 232 metals overhaul added). A fresh, line-item estimate per home and **per apartment unit** would be newly citable. Useful lines: framing, cabinets, vanities, countertops, tile, fixtures, windows, electrical gear.
- **DDP risk and refund pass-through.** DDP pricing moves duty-change risk to the seller. Buyers need clauses covering (a) who absorbs duty changes after the PO and (b) who keeps any duty refund, since IEEPA and Section 122 refunds go to the importer of record.
- **Sourcing is harder than "move out of China."** Vietnam now carries 12.5% (forced-labor 301) plus the new plywood AD and the quartz safeguard. Malaysia and Vietnam cabinets need non-China-component certification. Malaysia quartz faces evasion scrutiny. The "switch sourcing countries" advice in AI answers is oversimplified.
- **Multifamily finish packages are most exposed.** Developers carry the biggest tariff-exposed finish packages per building (cabinets, vanities, tops, tile, fixtures), and there is no multifamily-specific tariff content. This is Quotr's persona-content white space (consistent with the earlier audit).

- No per-apartment-unit or per-square-foot tariff estimate for multifamily was found.
- No 2026 NAHB builder-survey update on tariff cost per home was captured. nahb.org is blocked; only 2025 figures were seen.
- AGC's 2026 outlook survey results on tariffs (share of firms using escalation clauses, cancellations) were not retrieved.
- There is no quantitative data on how many builders are switching sourcing countries.

- **Accuracy is itself a differentiator.** Current AI answers contain material omissions. Quotr's content should show its reasoning with rates, sources and "as of" dates in tables, which AI engines favor (per the earlier audit's §3 citable-page features).

### Gaps
- Only Perplexity was tested; ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini, Claude and Copilot may cite different sources.
- No keyword or prompt-volume data was gathered for these questions.
- Reddit and forum questions (e.g., r/Construction, r/Contractor, r/HomeImprovement) were not sampled.
