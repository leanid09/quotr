## 1. What does the draft actually say? (plain-English summary of every material clause)

### Takeaway
The draft is a **generic, company-favorable consulting template** with three tailored parts: Section 9.2 (on-site days), Section 10 (exclusivity) and Schedule A (services). The original template seems to come from biotech or government work: it mentions "clinical data", "laboratory notebooks" and obligations to "the United States Government". It gives Quotr broad IP, an uncapped one-way indemnity, termination on 30 days' notice, and Leanid's exclusive services. It **lists no deliverable volumes or KPIs**, pays **in arrears**, and has **no limitation of liability, no background-IP carve-out and no portfolio right**.

## 3. Ranked risks to The Knowledge Company

### Takeaway
The top risks are an **exclusivity clause far broader than agreed**, an **IP clause that can capture TKC's core tools and methodology**, and the **lopsided combination of exclusivity with a 30-day exit and pay in arrears**. Close behind are an **uncapped one-way indemnity** and **contractor-classification problems that hurt both sides**. The first six are must-fix before signing.

### Cited Findings
Clause quotes are from [Draft 1][draft]; agreed terms are from the [thread][thread].

1. **CRITICAL, must fix: §10 exclusivity covers all clients, not just construction.** "neither the Consultant (with respect to Leanid Palkhouski's services) nor Leanid Palkhouski shall provide such services to any other company, entity or person". It is backed by instant termination for a "threatened" breach (§4) and an injunction without bond (§11). Leanid's inbox shows active non-construction GEO pipeline, for example a Photon GEO call in November 2026 (Gmail search results). Under §10, Leanid could not personally serve that client during the term without Quotr's written consent.
2. **CRITICAL, must fix: IP capture of TKC's business assets.**
   - §6.2(a)(i) assigns everything TKC creates during the term "if related to the business of the Company", not just work for Quotr.
   - §6.2(a)(ii) reaches post-term work "derived from Proprietary Information".
   - §6.1(b) defines Proprietary Information to include "know-how … methods, techniques" "developed" by TKC.
   - Schedule A declares "the growth system, playbooks … data and documentation" to be Inventions.
   - §6.2(b) grants Quotr a "perpetual, irrevocable, transferable … license with the right to … sublicense … sell … and otherwise exploit such Prior Inventions, without restriction".
   - The assignment is a present "hereby assigns", not conditional on payment.
   - Assets at risk include: the generic parts of the **geo-brain** knowledge base (GEO fundamentals, prompt library, playbooks, measurement method; first committed 2026-09-25, before the engagement, per the local repo history); TKC's AI content pipelines and agents; monitoring scripts; the reusable framework behind the **MCP connector**; research-repo templates; and TKC's GEO methodology as refined on Quotr.
3. **HIGH, must fix: one-sided commitment and payment exposure (§4, §3.1).**
   - Leanid is exclusive for 90 days, but Quotr can exit on 30 days' notice (§4(a)) or on 24 hours' notice for any alleged "material breach" with no cure period (§4(b)).
   - Payment is in arrears, so the first full payment arrives Dec 31.
   - Worst case: notice on Nov 30 ends the deal Dec 30 for about $5.5k total, after Leanid has turned away other work.
   - Worse case: around day 25 Quotr says the work fails "the Company's standards and approval" (§9.3) and terminates in 24 hours. At that point nothing has been paid, all IP is already assigned, and the "full settlement" sentence waives other claims.
4. **HIGH, must fix: §12 indemnity is uncapped and one-way, with no limitation of liability anywhere.** It covers "any nonfulfillment of any … responsibility" and "activities of the Consultant", with attorney fees. It would also cover competitor comparison articles (62 planned per [one-pager][onepager]) that Quotr approves and publishes. If an agency reclassifies the relationship, the §12 tax-indemnity sentence lets Quotr push "penalties, fees or charges" onto TKC.
5. **HIGH, should fix (affects both parties): contractor-classification signals.** The draft and Schedule A contain "general right of inspection and supervision" (§9.3), fixed on-site days, standups, "Growth ownership", the named individual Leanid, and all-client exclusivity. The exclusivity in particular cuts against the B2B-exemption factor that the provider can serve other clients without restriction (see Q5).
6. **MEDIUM-HIGH, must fix: team and subcontractor lockout (§1, §13.1).** No personnel are listed and subcontractors need Quotr's "sole discretion" consent. §13.1 warrants TKC "has provided to the Company all such agreements", which is likely false on the day of signing. It also requires TKC's staff to carry non-solicit covenants, which is questionable for California employees (see Q6).
7. **MEDIUM, should fix: undefined performance standard.** "the target" is undefined, combined with "best efforts" (§5) and "Company's standards and approval" (§9.3). Together these invite subjective breach claims.
8. **MEDIUM, should fix: all expenses fall on TKC (§3.2).** That includes SEO and AI-visibility tools, LLM and API costs, and any paid placements. The proposal excluded "third-party subscriptions, paid placements" ([one-pager][onepager]).
9. **MEDIUM, should fix: insurance (§13.4).** Workers' comp, motor vehicle and CGL at $1M, maintained for a year after the term. Leanid needs cover in place by Nov 30 and must show proof on request.
10. **MEDIUM, should fix: confidentiality.** It lasts forever, lacks the standard exclusions, has no portfolio or case-study right (§9.4 bars use of Quotr's name), and its no-removal rule (§6.1(d)) is unworkable for remote work.
11. **LOW-MEDIUM, nice to have: non-solicit (§7).** It runs for 6 months after the term, covers anyone "engaged by" Quotr, and does not protect TKC's own staff from being hired away.
12. **LOW, nice to have:** mail-only notices (§14); over-broad survival (§22); one-way remedies and waiver (§11, §23.1); no venue; template residue (§6.1(f)); the §2870 reference (see Q7).

### Inferences
- Risks 1-3 compound. **Broad exclusivity is tolerable only if Quotr is committed for 90 days and pays in advance.** If Quotr keeps the 30-day exit, exclusivity must shrink to construction competitors.
- Risk 2 is the one that can hurt TKC's business long after this deal. If Quotr holds a perpetual, sublicensable license to TKC's tooling "without restriction", it could in principle hand those tools to a cheaper agency or to an acquirer.

### Gaps
- It is unknown whether TKC is an LLC or a corporation, whether it has employees and how many, whether its staff have signed IP and confidentiality agreements, and whether TKC already carries CGL or E&O insurance. These answers change how severe items 6 and 9 are.

## 4. Proposed redline language and fallback positions

### Takeaway
There are about a dozen comments. Six are "must-fix": §10, §6.2 + §6.1(b) + Schedule A, §4, §3.1, §12 plus a new limitation-of-liability clause, and §1/§13.1. Each comment below is written so it can be pasted as a comment on the relevant clause without reformatting Quotr's document. Each has a fallback.

### Cited Findings
- The current clause text for each redline is quoted in Q1 above ([Draft 1][draft]).
- The agreed positions each redline restores are quoted in Q2 above ([E5][e5]; [one-pager][onepager]).

### Inferences
These are recommendations, not sourced facts. The language is suggested drafting for Leanid and his lawyer to adapt.

#### R1. §10 Exclusivity (MUST FIX)
**Proposed replacement:**
> 10. Exclusivity. During the Consultation Period, neither the Consultant nor Leanid Palkhouski will provide marketing, content, SEO, GEO, AEO or growth services to any Competitor. "Competitor" means any company whose primary business is construction takeoff, estimating or bidding software or services, or construction material procurement, including the companies listed in Schedule B. Nothing in this Agreement restricts the Consultant or Leanid Palkhouski from providing services to companies that are not Competitors, provided the Consultant meets its obligations under Schedule A. This Section 10 applies only during the Consultation Period and imposes no restriction following its expiration or termination. The Company retains a right to contract with other companies and/or individuals for consulting services without restriction.

- Schedule B should name STACK, PlanSwift, Togal.AI and Buildxact (the competitors named in geo-brain) plus any others Quotr wants.
- In §4 and §11, delete "or threatens to breach" and "or 10".

**Fallback 1:** widen to "any company in the construction sector, including construction technology, construction software and building-materials companies". This matches the Nov 19 wording exactly: "only construction-sector client".

**Fallback 2:** a time-based commitment instead of a client ban. "Leanid will reserve Mondays and Tuesdays for Quotr and will not take on any new client engagement that he personally leads requiring more than [X] hours per week", with existing clients listed in a schedule.

**Last resort:** accept all-client exclusivity **only if** Quotr accepts R2 Option A (no termination for convenience) **and** pays monthly in advance. Treat it as a priced "exclusivity premium".

#### R2. §4 Termination (MUST FIX)
**Option A, the mutual 90 days Leanid proposed.** Replace (a) with:
> (a) Neither party may terminate this Agreement for convenience before February 28, 2027.

**Option B fallback, a minimum term plus notice:**
> (a) by either party upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice, provided that no such notice may be given before December 29, 2026.

This guarantees about 60 days, roughly $11k.

**Option C fallback, an early-termination fee:**
> If the Company terminates under Section 4(a) before February 28, 2027, it shall pay, in addition to fees accrued through the effective date, an early-termination fee equal to fifty percent (50%) of the fees that would have been payable for the remainder of the Consultation Period.

## 5. California contractor-misclassification risk (AB5 / ABC test / B2B exemption / marketing exemption)

### Takeaway
The contract is between two businesses, so Quotr's best route to keep TKC a contractor is the **business-to-business (B2B) exemption, Labor Code §2776**. It requires Quotr to prove **all 12** criteria, after which the older multi-factor Borello test still applies. **Draft 1's all-client exclusivity, "supervision" language and fixed on-site days work against several of those criteria.** Narrowing exclusivity to competitors and framing on-site days as negotiated collaboration protects both sides. A secondary route, the "marketing" professional-services exemption in §2778, applies to individuals and has similar conditions.

### Cited Findings
- **All 12 criteria, burden on Quotr.** The B2B exemption requires that all 12 criteria in Labor Code §2776(a) be met, the hiring entity bears the burden on each, and failing any one disqualifies the arrangement ([AALRR][aalrr]; [LWDA B2B Requirement Chart][lwda]; [EDD DE 231BB][edd231bb]).
- **The 12 criteria in substance.** This is a paraphrase from §2776(a). The official text could not be opened in this session, and search results flagged that sources number the criteria differently, so verify against the statute ([public.law §2776][pl2776]; [LWDA chart][lwda]):
  1. free from the hiring business's control and direction, under the contract and in fact;
  2. provides services directly to the hiring business, not to its customers;
  3. **written contract specifying the payment amount or rate and the payment due date**;
  4. holds any **business license or business-tax registration** the local jurisdiction requires;
  5. maintains a **business location separate from the hiring business's** (a residence is allowed);
  6. customarily engaged in an independently established business of the same nature;
  7. **can contract with other businesses for the same or similar services and maintain a clientele without restrictions from the hiring entity**;
  8. advertises and holds itself out to the public;
  9. provides its own tools and equipment;
  10. can negotiate its own rates;
  11. **consistent with the nature of the work, can set or negotiate its own hours and location**;
  12. not doing work that requires a Contractors' State License Board licence.
- Search results confirm several of these criteria: a written contract stating "the payment amount and due date" ([sambrotman][sambrotman]), the provider being "free to serve other clients and advertise to the public" ([International Freelance Association][ifa]), and the freedom to set working hours ([sambrotman][sambrotman]).
- **Borello still applies.** Meeting the B2B criteria does not end the analysis: the hiring entity must still show contractor status under the common-law Borello test ([Barzilai][lbat]).
- **Marketing exemption.** Labor Code §2778 exempts "marketing" from the ABC test where the work is "original and creative in character and the result of which depends primarily on the invention, imagination, or talent of the individual" ([EDD DE 231PF][edd231pf]; [public.law §2778][pl2778]). Its individual-level conditions are, in substance: a separate business location, a business license, the ability to set or negotiate rates, the ability to set one's own hours outside project deadlines and reasonable business hours, being customarily engaged with other hiring entities **or** holding oneself out to them, and exercising discretion and independent judgment. This is a paraphrase to verify against [public.law §2778][pl2778].
- **Draft language that helps:** §9.2 says TKC has "the right to control and determine the time, place, methods, manner and means", is "not required to attend regular meetings", and "will provide all equipment and supplies". §3.1 gives a fixed rate and due date ([Draft][draft]).

Quotr's own opening position was explicitly employee-like: "operating as a full-time team member … attending all daily standups" ([E2][e2]).

## 6. Exclusivity and non-compete enforceability in California

### Takeaway
California voids **post-employment** non-competes with employees, and 2024 amendments added notice duties, a private right of action and "regardless of where signed" reach. But **§10 is an in-term restraint in a business-to-business contract**. Under *Ixchel Pharma v. Biogen* (2020), such restraints get a **rule-of-reason** review, not automatic voidness. Leanid should therefore **assume §10 is enforceable during the term** and negotiate it down rather than rely on it being void. The 6-month post-term **non-solicit** in §7 is the provision on weaker legal footing.

### Cited Findings
- **Ixchel Pharma v. Biogen.** In *Ixchel Pharma, LLC v. Biogen, Inc.* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 1130, the California Supreme Court held §16600 applies to business contracts but does not make all contractual restraints on business dealings void per se. Such restraints are subject to a rule of reason drawn from antitrust law, and exclusive-dealing and similar commercial agreements fail only if they fail that analysis ([Orrick][orrick]; [Pillsbury][pillsbury]; [MoFo][mofo]; [Paul Hastings][ph]).
- **Ixchel's limits.** It left intact California's general prohibition on post-employment restrictive covenants with employees ([MoFo][mofo]; [Littler][littler]).
- **SB 699 (B&P §16600.5, effective 2024).** Void non-competes are unenforceable "regardless of where and when the contract was signed", and employees, former employees and prospective employees get a private right of action with attorney fees ([Ogletree][ogletree]; [Goodwin][goodwin]).
- **AB 1076 (B&P §16600.1, effective 2024).** It is unlawful to include a non-compete clause in an employment contract. Employers had to notify affected employees by Feb 14, 2024. §16600 is read broadly in line with *Edwards v. Arthur Andersen* and is "not limited to contracts where the person being restrained … is a party" ([SGR][sgr]; [Goodwin][goodwin]).
- **Non-solicits.** Sources say neither SB 699 nor AB 1076 specifically addresses post-employment non-solicitation of employees or customers ([SGR][sgr] as summarized in search results).
- **Section 10 is in-term only.** It "applies only during the Consultation Period and imposes no restriction following its expiration or termination" ([Draft][draft]). Termination ends the Consultation Period (§2), so if Quotr exits early, exclusivity ends too.

### Inferences
- **During the term:** a 90-day exclusive-services covenant between two businesses, paid at $5.5k a month, would probably survive a rule-of-reason review, because its market effect is negligible. Leanid should not count on it being unenforceable. §11's injunction clause signals Quotr could seek to enforce it.
- **After the term:** §10 imposes nothing. §7's 6-month non-solicit is the only post-term restraint. California courts have been skeptical of employee non-solicits after *Edwards*, but this was not researched in depth here (lawyer). It is still better to narrow and mutualize §7 (R11) than to rely on it being void.
- **If Leanid were reclassified as an employee,** the employment-context rules in §§16600, 16600.1 and 16600.5 would govern any post-term restraint. That would favor Leanid, but reclassification is not a strategy.
- **§13.1(b) is a separate problem.** It requires TKC to put non-solicit covenants in its own California employees' agreements. Given AB 1076's prohibition on non-compete clauses in employment contracts, and uncertainty over whether employee non-solicits count, TKC should not take on that obligation. Ask to delete it (R6). Lawyer to confirm.

### Gaps
- No case law specific to in-term exclusivity for individual consultants or agencies, after *Ixchel*, was located.
- *AMN Healthcare v. Aya* and other employee non-solicit cases were not retrieved in this pass.
- The official text of §§16600, 16600.1 and 16600.5 could not be fetched (leginfo blocked).

## 7. IP assignment as a contractor: Labor Code §2870, the "work made for hire" trap, and background IP

### Takeaway
**§2870 protects employees, not contractors.** By its own terms, quoted in Exhibit A, it speaks of "an employment agreement" and "an employee". As a contractor, TKC's only protection is what the contract itself says. Draft 1's reference to §2870 does incorporate the carve-out contractually, but that carve-out excludes anything that "relate[s] … to the employer's business", which is everything TKC does for Quotr. So it protects almost nothing. The market-standard structure is: **assign the client-specific deliverables (ideally on payment), carve out and list the consultant's background IP, and grant the client a non-exclusive license limited to using the deliverables.** Draft 1 correctly avoids "work made for hire" language, and TKC should not accept any.

### Cited Findings
- **§2870 is drafted for employees.** Exhibit A's text: "Any provision in an **employment agreement** which provides that an **employee** shall assign … shall not apply to an invention that the employee developed entirely on his or her own time without using the employer's equipment … except for those inventions that either: (1) Relate … to the employer's business … or (2) Result from any work performed by the employee for the employer" ([Draft, Exhibit A][draft]).
- **Draft 1's use of §2870.** It assigns "to the maximum extent permitted by Section 2870" and states the assignment does "not apply to any Invention which qualifies fully under the provisions of California Labor Code Section 2870" ([Draft §6.2(a)][draft]).
- **Work-made-for-hire trap.** Labor Code §3351.5(c) treats a person engaged to create a commissioned work, under a signed writing calling it a "work made for hire", as an **employee** for workers' compensation purposes, with the commissioning party as employer. Commentators note related Unemployment Insurance Code provisions (§§621(d), 686) and recommend using an **assignment instead** of work-for-hire language ([Farella Braun + Martel][fbm]; [public.law §3351.5][pl3351]; [K.E. Mitchell][kem]).
- **Draft 1 has no work-for-hire language.** It uses "hereby assigns" ([Draft][draft]).
- **Background IP practice.** Practitioner guides recommend expressly identifying the consultant's pre-existing IP, typically in an exhibit, to prevent inadvertent assignment. Typical carve-outs are templates, methodologies and frameworks, software libraries, scripts and internal tools, pre-existing documentation and general know-how. A carve-out is often paired with a non-exclusive license so the client can use the deliverables. Guides also advise tying the timing of transfer to clearly defined deliverables and payment ([BCLP][bclp]; [Justia contract clause library][justiaclause]; [Aaron Hall][aaronhall]; [Queensland Govt IP kit][qld]).
- **Pre-existing TKC asset at stake.** The local repo holds a 68-page "Quotr.ai GEO Knowledge Brain" (`geo-brain/`), first committed 2026-09-25. It includes generic GEO fundamentals, a prompt library, playbooks, measurement and a 90-day plan, alongside Quotr-specific facts. Its README states it was "built from a September 2026 research audit" (local file `/home/user/quotr/geo-brain/README.md`; `git log`).

### Inferences
- Under Draft 1, geo-brain is a "Prior Invention" (it predates Nov 30). If any of it is "incorporated", Quotr gets the §6.2(b) perpetual, transferable, sublicensable license "without restriction". **Every update to it during the term that "relates to" Quotr's business would be assigned outright under §6.2(a)(i).** Schedule A then calls "playbooks" and the "growth system" Inventions. R4 splits this cleanly: Quotr owns Quotr-specific content, TKC keeps the framework.
- **The MCP connector and GitHub repositories:**
  - Quotr should own the Quotr-specific connector code and any repositories under Quotr's GitHub organization.
  - TKC's scaffolding, libraries and any repositories under TKC's own organization should be Consultant Materials.
  - Open-source components remain under their own licenses.
  - As drafted, §6.2(b)'s "will not incorporate any … proprietary information owned by any third party … without the Company's prior written permission" makes routine use of open-source and AI tools a technical breach unless pre-approved (R4(iii), R6).
- **"Hereby assigns" with no payment condition** means Quotr would own work it has not yet paid for, given that payment is in arrears. Assignment-on-payment (R4(i)) is a common consultant ask. If Quotr refuses, payment in advance (R3) largely cures the problem.
- **Don't let a "fix" add "work made for hire" language** (for example "all Deliverables shall be works made for hire"). Keep the assignment model.

### Gaps

## 8. Payment, termination and market-standard retainer terms (including the Freelance Worker Protection Act)

### Takeaway

Draft 1 flips this to payment in arrears and a 30-day exit for either side. ### Cited Findings
- **Proposal terms.** "Fee $5,500 a month, Monthly, in advance; 90-day total $16,500; 90-day initial commitment" ([one-pager][onepager]).

- **Freelance Worker Protection Act (SB 988).**
  - It defines a freelance worker as "a person or organization composed of no more than one person, whether or not incorporated or employing a tradename" ([Hinshaw][hinshaw]).
  - Payment is due on the date the contract sets, or, if none, within 30 days after the work is completed ([Fisher Phillips][fp]; [SJUD analysis][sjud]).
  - It applies to contracts entered into or renewed on or after Jan 1, 2025.
  - Hiring parties must keep the contract for at least four years.
  - It is enforced by the Labor Commissioner and the Attorney General, with a private right of action ([votesmart bill text][votesmart]; [Fisher Phillips][fp]).
  - The $250 written-contract threshold is reported by Freelancers Union but was not confirmed elsewhere ([Freelancers Union][fu]).
- **Calendar math (calculated).** Nov 30, 2026 to Feb 28, 2027 is exactly 90 days. Under Draft 1's calendar-month proration: Nov 30 earns about $183.33, then Dec 31, Jan 31 and Feb 28 each earn $5,500, for about $16,683. If Quotr gave 30-day notice on Nov 30, TKC would earn about $5.5k in total, and its first full payment would be due Dec 31 ([Draft][draft]).

### Inferences
- **Recommended position, in order:**
  1. Monthly in advance, invoiced at the start of each Service Month and paid within 5 business days, with the mutual 90-day commitment (R2-A, R3). This matches the proposal.
  2. Fallback: net 15 from an invoice issued at the start of each Service Month, plus a 60-day minimum (R2-B) or a 50% early-termination fee (R2-C).
  3. Floor: Quotr's 30-day exit with in-arrears payment is acceptable **only** if §10 is narrowed to construction competitors. Leanid should not give up other work for a commitment he can lose on 30 days' notice.
- **Kill fee.** A fee equal to 50% of the remaining fees, or at least one month, is a reasonable ask. It is not a sourced market statistic.
- **Notice periods.** 14–30 days of termination notice is common in agency retainers in practice, but no authoritative survey was found in this pass. Quotr's 30 days is acceptable **after** a minimum term.
- **Auto-renewal.** Avoid auto-renewal. Instead, add a month-to-month continuation by mutual written agreement after Feb 28, with exclusivity lapsing unless it is separately paid for (R12).
- **If TKC is in fact a one-person business** (Leanid plus AI tools and occasional freelancers), SB 988 may apply. It would add statutory backing to whatever payment date is written in, which is a reason to state explicit dates and amounts. Lawyer to confirm.

### Gaps
- No authoritative benchmark data on agency or consultant retainer payment terms, notice periods or kill fees was found. The figures above are negotiating judgments.
- The SB 988 text was read via secondary summaries. Its interaction with a multi-person agency was not confirmed.
