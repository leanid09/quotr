# Competitor GEO/Content Benchmark: AI Construction Takeoff, Estimating and Procurement Software (as of 2026-09-25)

Method note (applies to all sections): Live AI-answer tests were run on 2026-09-25 through `mcp__Slashy__web_search`, which is a Perplexity **Sonar** answer engine (the tool returns `"model": "sonar"`). Each prompt was run **once**; answers are non-deterministic, and ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini and Claude were NOT tested. `mcp__Slashy__scrape_url` returned "Rate limit exceeded. Retry after ~2820 seconds" on the first two calls, so competitor sitemaps, blog-index post counts, llms.txt files and on-page structure could not be read directly; evidence below comes from search-engine result listings (WebSearch) plus Perplexity answers and their citation lists. "Observed" = seen in a tool result; "Inferred" = my reasoning.

## 1. Competitor set: who "owns" AI answers in Quotr's category, and which competitors matter most to Quotr

### Takeaway
No single vendor owns the category. **Unbranded "best AI takeoff/estimating" answers are owned by Handoff (residential), STACK (GC all-rounder) and Togal.AI (AI-first takeoff), with Beam AI and Kreo as the next tier; Autodesk wins "multifamily"; Buildertrend/Buildxact win "bid leveling"; Procore/Trimble/Field Materials AI win "procurement software."** Quotr appears in AI answers only when the prompt names Quotr/its competitors or asks about factory-direct sourcing or outsourced-takeoff pricing. The most relevant competitors for Quotr are Togal.AI, Handoff, STACK, Beam AI, Kreo and Buildxact, plus a fast-growing swarm of AI-native newcomers (BuildVision AI, ContraVault, Easy Takeoffs, Foreman AI, OneEstimate, Meltplan, Exayard, Bobyard, xBuild) that publish "best X" listicles aggressively.

### Inferences
- Relevance ranking for Quotr (inferred from overlap of persona + product + AI visibility): (1) **Togal.AI** (AI takeoff benchmark every comparison anchors on), (2) **Handoff** (owns "residential" in AI answers and bundles Lowe's purchasing — closest to Quotr's estimating+procurement story for SFR), (3) **STACK** (default GC answer, huge review base), (4) **Beam AI** (AI + human-reviewed service = mirrors Quotr's Estimation Service), (5) **Kreo** (price anchor), (6) **Buildxact** (residential builders + dealer pricing + bid leveling), then Autodesk (multifamily), Bluebeam/PlanSwift/On-Screen Takeoff (incumbents people switch from). Houzz Pro, Destini, ProEst, Square Takeoff, Countfire, CostCertified barely surfaced in these tests and look lower-priority for GEO.
- Quotr's current AI visibility is "branded/comparison-only": it wins where it wrote the comparison itself or where the query is niche (factory-direct). It does not appear in any head-term, persona (residential, multifamily) or adjacent-category (bid leveling, procurement) answer tested.
- "Cited but not recommended" pattern (PlanSwift test) implies Quotr's listicles are trusted as neutral sources, but Quotr is not positioned inside them strongly enough (or engines discount self-promotion) to be named as a pick.

## 3. Which competitor pages AI answer engines cite, and what makes them citable

### Takeaway
Perplexity cites (a) **dated, table-heavy listicles with explicit prices** (Foreman AI "With Real Pricing", ContraVault, Handoff, Quotr), (b) **dated evergreen hubs from incumbents** (Bluebeam "Construction Takeoffs Guide 2026", Autodesk, Buildxact how-tos) for informational queries, (c) **review directories** (G2/Capterra alternatives pages) for "alternatives" queries, (d) **benchmark/number pages** (Meltplan "cost per square foot 2026 benchmarks") for cost questions, and (e) **authoritative institutions** (NAHB, JEC, Brookings) for macro topics like tariffs, where no software vendor was cited at all.

### Cited Findings
- Informational prompt "how do I do a quantity takeoff from PDF plans / what is AI takeoff" cited Bluebeam ([guide 2026](https://www.bluebeam.com/resources/construction-takeoffs-guide-2026/), [digital takeoff steps](https://blog.bluebeam.com/digital-takeoff-steps/)), Buildxact ([quantity takeoff](https://www.buildxact.com/us/blog/quantity-takeoff/)), BuildVision AI ([docs guide](https://www.buildvisionai.com/docs/guides/construction-takeoff-basics)), Houzz Pro ([takeoffs](https://pro.houzz.com/for-pros/takeoffs-construction)), Autodesk ([quantity takeoffs](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/quantity-takeoffs/)), ruh.ai ([guide](https://www.ruh.ai/industrial/construction/guides/how-to-do-a-construction-takeoff), [glossary](https://www.ruh.ai/industrial/construction/glossary/quantity-takeoff)), [openconstructionerp.com](https://openconstructionerp.com/cases/takeoff-quantities-from-a-pdf-plan). **Quotr's equivalent pages were not cited** even though they exist and rank in WebSearch: [quotr.ai/dictionary/ai-takeoff](https://quotr.ai/dictionary/ai-takeoff/), [quotr.ai/blog/how-to-do-construction-takeoff-pdf-blueprint](https://quotr.ai/blog/how-to-do-construction-takeoff-pdf-blueprint/).

### Inferences
- Citable = **(1) year in title and body ("2026") + recent update, (2) explicit numbers (prices, ranges, accuracy %, review counts with "as of" dates), (3) comparison tables and numbered steps, (4) quotable one-sentence definitions, (5) third-party-looking framing (covers rivals fairly), (6) domain authority for macro topics.**
- For head-term informational queries, incumbents' dated "guide 2026" hubs plus AI-native docs pages (BuildVision) beat Quotr's dictionary/how-to pages; likely factors are domain authority, backlinks and third-party mentions rather than page existence (not verifiable here).
- Cost-benchmark pages are a proven wedge for small AI-native vendors (Meltplan, Exayard get cited next to RSMeans) — a format Quotr can replicate with its own estimating and factory pricing data.
- On tariff/landed-cost questions, engines currently fall back to government and media sources — a vendor with genuine, dated, first-party price data could become the "practitioner" source.
