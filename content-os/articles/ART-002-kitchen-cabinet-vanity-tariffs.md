# Kitchen cabinet and vanity tariffs: what changes on January 1, 2027 and what builders should do now

By [AUTHOR: Hanyang Liu, CEO] · Published: November 16, 2026 · Last updated: November 16, 2026

Not legal or customs advice; reviewed by [EXPERT REVIEWER — required before publish]. Tariff status checked: November 13, 2026. Confirm rates for your own products and suppliers with a licensed customs broker.

**Quick answer:** Kitchen cabinets and vanities imported into the US carry a 25% Section 232 tariff, in effect since October 14, 2025. That rate is scheduled to rise to 50% on January 1, 2027, after a one-year delay. Cabinets and vanities from China also face separate anti-dumping and countervailing duties (AD/CVD), in place since 2020, with rates set per manufacturer. Before January 1, check your classification, your supplier's AD/CVD rate and your shipping dates.

## What tariffs apply to kitchen cabinets and vanities from China right now?

Several separate duties can apply to the same cabinet from China, and they add up: the Section 232 tariff, AD/CVD, and Section 301 duties on Chinese goods [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH: stacking, per Covington's April 2026 alert]. Customs fees come on top.

*Table: duties on kitchen cabinets and vanities from China, status as of November 13, 2026.*

| Duty | What it covers | Rate today | Scheduled change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 232 (wood products) | Kitchen cabinets, vanities and their parts, from all countries | 25% since October 14, 2025 | 50% from January 1, 2027; may not apply to countries that reach an agreement with the US |
| AD/CVD orders on wooden cabinets and vanities from China | Cabinets, vanities and components made in China, including flat-pack | Set per exporter and updated in annual reviews; China-wide AD rate reported at 251.64% in final results published April 23, 2026 [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH] | Orders continued in September 2025 after a five-year review |
| Section 301 (China) | Many Chinese goods | [EXPERT REVIEWER: confirm whether and at what rate it applies to these HTS codes] | None announced in our sources |
| Customs user fees | Most formal entries | Merchandise processing fee 0.3464% (per-entry minimum and maximum apply); harbor maintenance fee 0.125% for ocean cargo [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH] | Adjusted periodically |

HTS codes named in the Section 232 coverage include 9403.40.9060, 9403.60.8093 and 9403.91.0080 [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH with a broker]. Look up any code in the [USITC tariff schedule search](https://hts.usitc.gov/).

The Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that the IEEPA emergency law does not authorize tariffs, but that ruling did not reach Section 232 or Section 301 duties. The cabinet tariff still applies.

## What exactly changes on January 1, 2027?

The Section 232 rate on kitchen cabinets and vanities is scheduled to double, from 25% to 50%. As of November 13, 2026, we found no announcement moving the date again, and a November 5, 2026 Reuters report still described the increase as set for January 1 [VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISH, and again before January 1].

In general, the rate that applies to a shipment depends on when it is entered into the US, not when you ordered or paid for it. [EXPERT REVIEWER: confirm, and confirm whether any in-transit provision applies to the January 1 increase.]

Cabinets and vanities stand out because two kinds of duty hit Chinese product at once. Other materials named as tariff-exposed for home builders include lumber and plywood, steel, aluminum and copper (CNBC; HousingWire).

## How do you calculate the landed cost of imported cabinets?

Landed cost is the full cost of imported goods once they reach you: product price + freight and insurance + duties + customs fees + broker, port and delivery charges. Two shipping terms decide who pays what:

- **FOB (free on board):** the seller's price covers the goods loaded on the ship at the origin port. Freight, insurance, US customs, duties and delivery are the buyer's.
- **DDP (delivered duty paid):** the seller's price covers everything to your named delivery point, including US customs clearance and duties.

| Cost | FOB | DDP |
|---|---|---|
| Product, export packing, loading at origin | Seller | Seller |
| Ocean freight and insurance | Buyer | Seller |
| US customs entry, broker, ISF filing | Buyer | Seller |
| Section 232, AD/CVD and other duties | Buyer | Seller |
| Port, trucking, delivery to jobsite | Buyer | Seller, to the named place |
| Risk if a duty rate changes before entry | Buyer | Seller, unless the contract passes it back |

Duties are generally charged on the customs value, which for most purchases is the price paid for the goods, without international freight and insurance [EXPERT REVIEWER: confirm]. Some freight forwarders' guides note that FOB is the more common term for building materials and warn buyers to check how duties are declared under DDP. Either way, ask for the duty math in writing.

## What should builders do before January 1?

1. **Count what is exposed.** List every cabinet and vanity line, with quantities, in open bids and signed jobs.
2. **Confirm HTS codes with a licensed customs broker.** Classification decides which duties apply.
3. **Check AD/CVD exposure for your exact manufacturer.** Ask for its AD/CVD case numbers and current cash-deposit rates in writing. A company-specific rate can be very different from the China-wide rate.
4. **Work back from the entry date.** Add production time, trucking to the port, ocean transit and customs entry. From November 16, there are 46 days to January 1. If your shipment cannot be entered by then, budget at 50%.
5. **Get the duty assumption in writing.** For any DDP quote, ask which Section 232 rate the price assumes and who pays if the rate changes before entry.
6. **Protect your bids.** Add tariff or escalation language to proposals and contracts that span January 1. Our guide to [tariff-aware estimating](https://quotr.ai/blog/tariff-aware-estimating-material-escalation-every-bid/) covers clause options.
7. **Collect compliance documents.** Cabinets made with composite wood (hardwood plywood, MDF, particleboard) must meet the CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI formaldehyde standards, and importers certify TSCA compliance at entry [EXPERT REVIEWER: confirm]. Get labels and certificates before goods ship.
8. **Price a domestic option too.** At 50% plus AD/CVD, a US-made or non-Chinese cabinet may win on landed cost. Changing the country of origin does not avoid Section 232, which applies to all countries unless an agreement says otherwise.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are bathroom vanities taxed the same as kitchen cabinets?

Yes. Vanities and their parts fall under the same Section 232 action as kitchen cabinets. Both have carried a 25% tariff since October 14, 2025, scheduled to rise to 50% on January 1, 2027. Vanities from China are also covered by the anti-dumping and countervailing duty orders on wooden cabinets and vanities.

### Do anti-dumping duties stack on top of the Section 232 tariff?

Yes. Anti-dumping and countervailing duties on Chinese wooden cabinets and vanities are charged in addition to the Section 232 tariff, and Section 301 duties on Chinese goods can also apply. AD/CVD rates are set per manufacturer, so a licensed customs broker should confirm the full duty stack for your HTS code and supplier.

### Did the Supreme Court tariff ruling remove the cabinet tariff?

No. The Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that the IEEPA emergency law does not authorize tariffs. The cabinet and vanity tariff was imposed under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, which the ruling did not cover.

### Does ordering before January 1 lock in the 25% rate?

Not by itself. In general, the duty rate depends on when the goods are entered into the United States, not when you place or pay for the order. Ask your customs broker how the January 1 change applies to goods already in transit.

### What is the difference between DDP and FOB?

Under FOB (free on board), the seller's price covers the goods loaded at the origin port, and the buyer pays freight, US customs, duties and delivery. Under DDP (delivered duty paid), the seller's price covers everything to the named delivery point, including customs clearance and duties.

Context: reply to a thread like "Anyone know what's happening with the cabinet tariff next year? Client wants imported cabinets."

> Short version, as of mid-November 2026 (not a customs broker, so get yours to confirm):
>
> - Imported kitchen cabinets and vanities have a 25% Section 232 tariff since Oct 14, 2025. It's scheduled to go to 50% on Jan 1, 2027. It was already delayed once (it was supposed to be Jan 1, 2026).
> - If they're from China, there are also anti-dumping/countervailing duties that have been around since 2020. Those are set per manufacturer, and some rates are very high. Ask the supplier for their specific rate in writing. This is the number people forget.
> - The rate generally depends on when the cabinets are entered into the US, not when you order. If production plus shipping puts entry after Dec 31, price it at 50%.
> - If you're getting a "DDP" (delivered duty paid) price, ask which tariff rate it assumes and who eats it if the rate changes. Get that in the contract.
> - Put tariff/escalation language in your contract with the client either way.
>
> Also worth pricing a domestic line side by side. Moving to another country of origin doesn't avoid the 232 tariff.
>
