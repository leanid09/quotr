## How do you calculate savings from a factory-direct quote?

Savings is the market price minus the price paid. Savings percentage is that difference divided by the market price. Because Quotr gives each market price as a range, every project has a low and a high figure.

- **Saratoga, low end:** $187,000 − $97,000 = $90,000. $90,000 ÷ $187,000 = 48.1%.
- **Saratoga, high end:** $218,000 − $97,000 = $121,000. $121,000 ÷ $218,000 = 55.5%.
- **Monte Sereno:** $195,000 − $108,290 = $86,710, or 44.5%; $245,000 − $108,290 = $136,710, or 55.8%.
- **Hillsborough:** $58,000 − $30,437 = $27,563, or 47.5%; $76,000 − $30,437 = $45,563, or 60.0%.
- **All three:** $440,000 − $235,727 = $204,273, or 46.4%; $539,000 − $235,727 = $303,273, or 56.3%.

Watch the base. Saratoga's market range was 93% to 125% higher than what was paid ($187,000 ÷ $97,000 = 1.93; $218,000 ÷ $97,000 = 2.25). That describes the same numbers as "48.1% to 55.5% less". Both are true, but only the second tells a buyer what share of the local price they keep.

## Why are per-project numbers more honest than "up to 50% off"?

A single headline percentage hides most of what a buyer needs to know. These three projects show why:

- **The spread is wide.** Savings ran from 44.5% to 60.0% across three homes in one region. One number cannot describe that.
- **"Up to" only describes the best case.** It tells you nothing about the typical project.
- **The comparison price moves the result.** Using the low or high end of the local range changes each project's figure by 7.4 to 12.5 percentage points. The low end is the conservative number to plan with: 44.5% to 48.1% here.
- **Three projects is a small sample.** All three are homes in Santa Clara and San Mateo counties. A different region, finish level or product mix can come out differently.
- **Timing changes the math.** The US tariff on imported kitchen cabinets and vanities has been 25% since October 14, 2025 and is scheduled to rise to 50% on January 1, 2027, and Chinese cabinets also carry anti-dumping and countervailing duties. A comparison made before October 2025 may not hold today [TO CONFIRM: when each project was bought].

If any supplier, including us, quotes a single savings percentage, ask for the project-level numbers behind it, with both prices and what each one included.

1. **Lead time, door to door.** Get the quote time and the delivery date in writing. For Quotr Procurement: [TO CONFIRM: quote and delivery lead times].
2. **Quality control.** Who inspects, when, and against which specification? Will you see photos or video before shipment? Quotr says it handles sampling and quality control [TO CONFIRM: process details].
3. **Samples.** Can you approve a physical sample of each finish before production?

## How else can you reduce building material costs?

Factory-direct buying is one lever. Others cost nothing to try:

- **Measure before you buy.** An accurate takeoff cuts over-ordering and emergency reorders.
- **Standardize finishes** across units or homes, so you buy fewer products in larger quantities.
- **Get three comparable quotes** on the same written scope.
- **Buy long-lead and tariff-exposed items early,** and put escalation language in contracts.
- **Value-engineer where the owner won't notice,** such as substrate and hidden hardware, not the visible finishes.
- **Keep commodity and urgent items local** (lumber, drywall, last-minute replacements), and import finish packages only where the schedule allows.

## When is factory-direct not the right choice?

- The schedule is too tight for production plus ocean shipping.
- The order is small or a one-off replacement.
- You need a local showroom, local warranty service or on-site adjustments.
- The product is a Chinese-made cabinet whose anti-dumping rate, plus the Section 232 tariff, wipes out the price gap.
- You cannot check specifications, certifications or quality control before shipment.

In those cases, buy locally and use the comparison table above to keep the local quote honest.

### What does delivered duty-paid (DDP) mean for building materials?

DDP means the seller's price covers getting the goods to your named delivery point, including ocean freight, US customs clearance and import duties. Under FOB, the buyer takes over those costs once the goods are loaded at the origin port.

### Why do savings claims for factory-direct materials vary so much?

Because each claim depends on what it is compared with. A comparison against the high end of local quotes looks better than one against the low end, scope differences such as delivery, tax or installation change the result, and tariff changes make older comparisons out of date. Project-level numbers with both prices shown are the most useful.

### Is buying factory-direct from China still worth it after the 2026 tariffs?

Sometimes. Tariffs raise the landed cost: kitchen cabinets and vanities carry a 25% Section 232 tariff that is scheduled to rise to 50% on January 1, 2027, and Chinese cabinets also face anti-dumping and countervailing duties. Compare delivered, duty-paid prices against local quotes for the same scope, category by category, before deciding.
