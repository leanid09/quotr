### As drafted, the deal gives Quotr employee-level control and TKC contractor-level security

The worst realistic case is concrete. Suppose Quotr gives notice on Nov 30. The deal then ends Dec 30, having paid about $5,500, and the first full payment is not due until Dec 31. By then all IP is already assigned, because the assignment is a present "hereby assigns" that does not wait for payment, and Leanid has turned away other work under §10. A harsher version uses §9.3's subjective "standards and approval" to allege material breach. §4(b) lets Quotr act on that with 24 hours' notice and no cure period, and the final payment then "shall constitute full settlement of any and all claims" ([Draft 1](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1V6krUVs9oKJAwDhAUFZfrG2AUumSYhK8cqm55MSgIeY/edit)). Leanid's inbox already holds the kind of work §10 would block: a Nov 20 GEO call with Photon, an AI-messaging infrastructure company outside construction ([Photon briefing](https://mail.google.com/mail/?authuser=leanid@knowledgecompany.ai#all/thread-f:1882424819032051454)).

Three legal points shape how hard to push. None of this is legal advice, and the statute texts could not be opened directly.

**1. Assume §10 is enforceable during the term.** Since *Ixchel Pharma v. Biogen* (2020), California reviews in-term restraints between businesses under a rule of reason instead of voiding them outright ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2020/08/The-California-Supreme-Court-Clarifies-Section-16600-as-Applied-to-Business-Contracts-and-Holds-Tha); [MoFo](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/200807-california-supreme-court)). The 2024 anti-non-compete statutes are aimed at employment contracts ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/01/alerts-practices-tsemnc-new-laws-reinforce-californias-hostility-to-non-competes-with-notice)).

**2. §10 hurts Quotr too.** Quotr's cleanest way to keep TKC classified as a contractor is California's business-to-business exemption (Labor Code §2776). Under it, Quotr must prove all 12 criteria, including that the provider can work for other businesses and keep its own clients without restriction from the hiring entity ([LWDA B2B chart](https://www.labor.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/338/2025/03/B2B-Requirement-Chart.pdf); [AALRR](https://aalrr.com/Labor-Employment-Law-Blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-the)). All-client exclusivity, a "general right of inspection and supervision" and fixed on-site days all work against that test [Likely; lawyer to confirm]. This gives Leanid a non-adversarial argument: narrowing §10 protects Quotr's classification as much as it protects his business.

**3. Labor Code §2870 (quoted in Exhibit A) protects employees, not contractors.** TKC's only protection is whatever carve-out the contract itself contains. Market practice is to assign client-specific deliverables, list the consultant's background IP, and license that IP only for use inside the deliverables ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/contracting-with-consultants-how-to-protect-your-ip-rights.html)). Leanid should refuse any "fix" that adds work-made-for-hire language, because California can treat that language as creating employee status for workers' compensation ([Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/employment/publications/employment-law-issues-to-consider-before-including-work-made-for-hire-clauses-in-contractor-agreements/)).

## Regional plays should sell cost data by place, never software by state

**How AI engines treat location.** They localize **local-business** questions, not B2B software recommendations. ChatGPT search uses approximate IP location for local results and works better when the user names a place ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search)). In one 36-prompt test, a map of local providers appeared on 12 of 12 prompts that named a city and on 3 of 12 that did not ([Scaile](https://scaile.tech/blog/fundamentals/mechanics/how-chatgpt-surfaces-local-businesses)). A web search for state-specific construction estimating software returned only national listicles. No study shows engines changing SaaS recommendations by US state [Known gap].

**Where location does matter.** Country and language change brand lists sharply. In one study, 159 of 206 brands appeared in only one country ([MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/fta-global-launches-context-report/)), and the language of the query changes which brands get recommended ([SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/lost-in-translation-how-language-changes-which-brand-ai-recommends/591585)). Regional **cost, material-price and landed-cost** questions genuinely differ by place, and Quotr holds proprietary data on exactly those [Likely]. That is the regional white space.

**What Google allows.** The spam policy's doorway examples include pages "targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). John Mueller called about 1,300 city landing pages doorways ([SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-city-landing-pages-doorway-pages-28670.html)). Practitioners advise building the fewest geographic pages needed ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/multi-location-seo-structure-geographic-pages-483959)) and making more than half of each page unique ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-seo/location-pages/)). The model to copy is RSMeans-style location cost data, which covers 930+ locations and is updated quarterly ([RSMeans](https://www.rsmeans.com/media/wysiwyg/product_pdf/2026-SQFT-TOCs.pdf)), with transparent methods. Angi-style city folders are the wrong model.

**Three gates before any regional page ships.** First, is Quotr's "costs by US zip code" database its own data or licensed? If licensed, publishing it may breach the license and would be commodity content anyway. Second, does Procurement deliver outside California? The site says both "your CA jobsite" and "coast to coast", and every non-California factory-direct comparison depends on the answer. Third, which HQ fact is correct? An inconsistent HQ would spread onto every regional page.

**Reconciling the proposal's four markets.** Bay Area and Los Angeles become metro pages under California. Florida stays. Texas should be added: it led 2025 US housing permits with 210,217 units, against 178,297 for Florida and 103,856 for California ([Census](https://www.census.gov/construction/bps/pdf/state_chart.pdf)), and Quotr exhibited at Dallas Build Expo.

## Conclusion

The proposal and the contract have the same flaw in mirror image. Leanid's proposal over-promises things that are easy to count and hard to make valuable. Quotr's template under-protects him on everything he would need to deliver real value. Schedule A is the single lever that fixes both, because Quotr has already shown it will copy his language into it. A Schedule A that lists realistic deliverables, a defined target, Quotr's dependencies and an approval-timeout counting rule removes the PDF's 310-asset yardstick and the contract's undefined "target" at the same time. Every other redline is standard agency hygiene for a startup reusing a biotech template, although the liability cap and the scope of the IP license will probably need a second round with Quotr's counsel.

The GEO lesson is the same one in different form. Quotr's bottleneck has never been ideas or volume; it is implementation (2 of 14 September fixes shipped) and corroboration (3 reviews, no directory listings, conflicting facts). The most valuable thing TKC can sell in these 90 days is ownership of the work that does not need Jaron: off-site profiles, reviews, list outreach, fact governance and honest measurement. That should be paired with one data asset that makes AI say "Quotr.ai" next to construction cost numbers it already repeats anonymously. If, by late February, branded answers quote $79.90, a handful of genuine reviews and listings exist, and the unbranded mention rate has doubled against a protocol Quotr signed, Leanid will have a renewal case built on evidence rather than article counts.
