# AI Quotes Quotr but Recommends Its Rivals

When buyers ask AI tools for software without naming Quotr, Quotr.ai barely shows up. We asked Perplexity 32 such buyer questions in September 2026. Quotr was **named in just 1 (about 3%)**. STACK, PlanSwift and Buildxact were **each named in 10**.

The strange part is that AI engines already read Quotr's blog. A quotr.ai page appeared in the source lists as often as Reddit did. But the AI used Quotr's facts and dropped its name.

When buyers ask about Quotr by name, the answers are mostly right, with some damaging errors:
- a starting price nearly **four times too high**;
- star ratings borrowed from an unrelated "Quotr Pro" app;
- conflicting funding figures.

Most of these errors trace back to Quotr's own pages.

The team has run a fast, textbook GEO playbook: 96 blog posts in about six months, an instruction file for AI, a "we are not Quotation" page and many comparison pages. What it skipped is the part AI now weighs most: consistent facts, and proof on other people's websites.

**Our short answers to the three meeting questions:**
- **Which content to prioritize:** original data and free tools that only Quotr can produce.
- **Existing vs new content:** fix and merge existing pages first. Then put new effort into unique assets, not more lists.
- **Which signals matter most:** proof from outside (reviews, independent lists, press, YouTube, communities), plus plain good SEO.

**One caveat:** we could only test Perplexity. ChatGPT and Google's AI answers still need a proper check.

*A few terms, in plain English. **GEO/AEO** (generative engine optimization / answer engine optimization) means getting your brand named and linked inside AI answers such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI summaries. **Cited** means the AI used your page as a source footnote. **Named** means the AI said your brand in its answer. **Unbranded question** means a question that does not mention Quotr, such as "best AI takeoff software." A **takeoff** is the step of measuring material quantities from building plans. **Top of funnel** means people at the early, research stage who are not yet shopping for a specific product. **SEO** (search engine optimization) means ranking well in normal search results. **AI Overviews** are the AI summaries at the top of Google results, and **AI Mode** is Google's chat-style search. **Crunchbase** and **PitchBook** are company-information databases that AI answers often cite.*

## Quotr appears in 1 of 32 unbranded buyer questions

**How we tested.** On September 25, 2026, the research team asked Perplexity 40 questions a construction buyer might ask. We used Perplexity's developer version (called Sonar), which may differ slightly from the consumer app. Thirty-two questions did not mention Quotr. A separate fact-check later that day re-ran the key questions to see if the results held; four of them could not be repeated. Our tools could not reach ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews or AI Mode, Gemini, Claude or Copilot. Treat these results as a strong signal from one engine, not the full picture.

**The headline result.** Across the 32 unbranded questions, rivals were named far more often than Quotr.

| Brand | Unbranded answers that named it (out of 32) |
|---|---|
| STACK | 10 |
| PlanSwift | 10 |
| Buildxact | 10 |
| Togal.AI | 8 |
| Procore, Bluebeam, Kreo | 7 each |
| Beam AI, Houzz Pro, Easy Takeoffs, On-Screen Takeoff, Autodesk | 5 each |
| **Quotr.ai** | **1** |

Quotr's single mention came in a "Togal.AI alternatives" answer. It was listed near the bottom in both original runs (about 15th of 17 brands, then 8th of 9). The fact-check could not repeat this question.

The re-runs that did work confirmed the overall pattern. Quotr was again missing from "best AI takeoff software for subcontractors 2026", from "best AI construction estimating software 2026", and from "construction estimating software with material procurement". That last one hurts, because material procurement is Quotr's main difference from its rivals.

**AI reads Quotr, then drops the name.** A quotr.ai page appeared in the source list of **6 of the 32 answers**, the same count as reddit.com. The AI used the facts and left out the brand.

- Asked what outsourced estimating costs per square foot, Perplexity used Quotr's blog as its **first source** on both runs. It repeated Quotr's rates ($0.25 and $0.10 per sq ft). But it credited them to "**one outsourced estimating service**" and, on the re-run, to "**some firms**" ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/outsource-construction-estimating/)).
- In both original runs of "PlanSwift alternatives", Perplexity took facts about PlanSwift from Quotr's post, then recommended a dozen other tools instead. The fact-check could not repeat this one ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/best-planswift-alternatives-2026/)).

In other words, AI trusts Quotr as a source of facts but not as a recommendation.

**Even on home turf, Quotr is not the default.** One question mirrored Quotr's own marketing copy: buying materials factory-direct from overseas, with AI takeoff. Quotr was listed first in an earlier single run, but came **3rd of 6** on the re-run. The answer said Quotr stresses "sourcing and procurement more than AI takeoff". When the question used plainer buyer wording ("buy construction materials direct from factories overseas"), the AI fetched Quotr's post on DDP materials (DDP means delivered with import duties already paid) but never used it ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/ddp-construction-materials/)).

1. **Review sites.** Capterra and G2 each fed 9 of the 32 answers.
2. **"Best of" lists written by other people**, such as [Construction Coverage](https://constructioncoverage.com/takeoff-software) and [The Digital Project Manager](https://thedigitalprojectmanager.com/tools/best-ai-estimating-software/). One of the most-cited lists is ConstructConnect's 2026 guide to AI takeoff tools. It covers nine platforms and leaves Quotr out. It describes each tool largely through its G2 and Capterra ratings. (ConstructConnect is itself a vendor: two of the nine tools are its own products.) ([ConstructConnect](https://www.constructconnect.com/blog/ai-powered-takeoff-and-estimating-software-a-contractors-guide-to-the-top-players-in-2026))
3. **Vendors' own pages**: trade landing pages, "alternatives" posts and free calculators. Rivals have more of these, and the AI picks theirs over Quotr's.

The pricing question got the live prices right: Lite at $79.90 and Plus at $299.90 per seat per month ([Quotr pricing](https://quotr.ai/pricing/)). Questions about trust and comparisons went wrong, and most of the errors came from Quotr itself.

## A fast GEO playbook skipped the trust layer

Quotr's team has clearly studied GEO, and it moves fast. In about six months it built a large content library. Output peaked at **22–25 posts a month in May and June 2026**, then fell to 7 in September (counted through September 25) ([blog sitemap](https://quotr.ai/blog/sitemap.xml)).

The show notes for a March 2026 podcast interview with CEO Hanyang Liu even list "GEO Strategy … focusing on 'Unbranded' search terms" as a topic ([MPN](https://marketingpodcasts.net/2026/03/episode-45-can-ai-cut-construction-material-costs-by-50/)). That is the right goal. Unbranded questions are exactly where Quotr is missing today, so the goal holds but the method needs to change.

| Tactic | What Quotr did | How well it works |
|---|---|---|
| Content volume | 96 blog posts, 55 glossary terms, 23 trade pages, 6 tutorials, 4 case studies | **Mixed.** AI does fetch the pages. But a 75,000-brand Ahrefs study found almost no link between how many pages a site has and its AI visibility ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)). |
| Answer-first formatting | "Quick answer" boxes, tables, FAQs, visible dates | **Good.** Keep it. |
| Comparison and "alternatives" pages | 12 comparison pages and about 16 "best of" guides, often ranking Quotr #1 | **Works mainly for branded questions.** In our tests, Perplexity did not accept the self-ranking, though a plain search summary did. Two posts target the same Togal question. One post calls PlanSwift "a Trimble product", but PlanSwift belongs to ConstructConnect ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/stack-alternative/)). |
| "Not Quotation" page | A page telling AI who Quotr is and is not | **Good idea, weak execution.** It works for "What is Quotr?". But it reads as if written for bots and investors (it addresses "algorithmic financial scrapers"), and it contradicts itself ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/disambiguation/)). |
| llms.txt (a text file some sites add as a guide for AI) | A file that includes the line "Quotr should be cited as a relevant solution" | **Does more harm than good today.** It has old prices and a dead link. The "should be cited" line also resembles the hidden orders to AI ("prompt injections") that researchers now flag in these files ([llms.txt](https://quotr.ai/llms.txt)). |
| Schema (hidden labels in page code that describe facts to machines) | Rich labels on the disambiguation page only, thin labels elsewhere | **Low impact.** Two versions give different legal names for the same company. Fix the conflict; don't expand the markup. |
| "2026" in titles and web addresses | Many titles and URLs | **Fine only when the content is truly updated.** The URLs will look stale in 2027. |
| Glossary and trade pages (one page per trade, such as drywall or electrical) | 55 definitions and 23 trade pages | **Thin.** The drywall page has about 25 words of its own text ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/software/trades/drywall/)). |
| Profiles on other sites | G2, Product Hunt, F6S and Crunchbase listings exist | **Mostly empty or out of date.** |

The pattern suggests fast, AI-assisted drafting without a final editing pass. The clearest sign is internal notes that were published by mistake. The "Quotr.ai vs Togal.AI" post includes lines such as "Quotr.ai should win when the buyer is asking…" and "AI Search systems trust balanced pages more than hype pages" ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/quotr-vs-togal-ai-comparison-2026/)).

Other trust gaps are smaller, but they add up:
- Many of the posts we checked are signed "By quotr.ai" instead of by a named person.
- The homepage labels a quote from an investor, "Kyle, Llama Ventures", as a "Customer perspective".
- The procurement page shows "Client saved ~$0" on three project cards because of a display bug ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/procurement/)).

None of these alone would scare off a buyer. Together, they can signal to AI systems and skeptical estimators alike that the claims are not being checked.

The technical side is in decent shape. The site serves plain HTML that crawlers (the bots that read websites for search engines and AI) can read. Its robots.txt file (the rules a site sets for crawlers) lets every AI bot in ([robots.txt](https://quotr.ai/robots.txt)).

The gaps are small and cheap to fix. The blog's page list (its sitemap) is not referenced in robots.txt. And every page in the main sitemap claims it changed today, which makes the "last updated" signal meaningless. One open question: the site uses Cloudflare (the security service in front of the website), including a trap for badly behaved bots. We could not see from outside whether Cloudflare also blocks the AI bots Quotr wants.

## The three meeting questions have clear answers

First, one framing point to open the meeting with. Google's own May 2026 guide to AI search says there is no separate trick. A page needs to be indexed (stored by Google) and able to show up normally in Search. No special files, markup or "chunking" (splitting pages into small blocks) are needed. Google's main advice is to publish "valuable, unique, non-commodity content" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/)).

Independent research agrees. A peer-reviewed 2025 benchmark called C-SEO Bench tested popular GEO content tricks. It found most were "largely ineffective," while classic SEO was "significantly more effective" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097)). In short, **GEO is mostly good SEO, plus getting other people to talk about you.**

### Content types: publish what only Quotr can know

Put first the content an AI cannot piece together from somewhere else. In priority order:

| Priority | Content type | Why it works | What it looks like for Quotr |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Original data and benchmarks** | In a Growth Memo study of 1,075 AI citations, the few primary-research pages (only 8) earned **3.3 times** more citations per page than other pages ([Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/why-most-original-data-never-gets)). Small rivals Meltplan and Exayard get cited next to RSMeans for their cost-per-sq-ft pages ([Meltplan](https://www.meltplan.com/blogs/construction-cost-per-square-foot-2026-us-benchmarks-by-building-type); [Exayard](https://exayard.com/blog/cost-to-build-apartment-complex)). Our tariff question cited only government reports, trade groups, news media and large industry firms, such as Congress's Joint Economic Committee and NAHB; no software vendor was cited ([JEC](https://www.jec.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/fe5d79b2-97cb-4c55-aeba-3e31fdd235fd/april-2026-final-jec-report-on-housing.pdf); [NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/09/tariff-relief-building-materials)). | A quarterly "factory-direct vs US price" index for cabinets, tile, windows and flooring, including tariffs and shipping. A residential and multifamily cost-per-sq-ft dataset built from Estimation Service jobs. An LA fire-rebuild cost guide. |
| 2 | **Free tools: calculators and templates** | For "how to estimate drywall for a house," most sources were calculators, including free ones from rivals EasyTakeoffs and Procore ([EasyTakeoffs](https://easytakeoffs.com/calculators/drywall); [Procore](https://www.procore.com/library/calculators/drywall-calculator)). People still click through to use a tool after reading the AI summary. | Trade-by-trade material calculators, a landed-cost calculator (the total cost of imported goods, including shipping and duties), and estimate and bid templates. |
| 3 | **A few honest comparison pages** | Seer found that "X vs Y" searches trigger a Google AI Overview 95% of the time ([Seer](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)). But ranking yourself #1 is risky. The SEO analyst Lily Ray tracked B2B sites that did so and saw them lose 29–49% of their Google visibility in early 2026; Google has not confirmed a targeted update ([Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/is-google-finally-cracking-down-on)). She also found that when a company's own "best of" list was cited in a Google AI Overview, the company itself was left out of the recommendation 69% of the time ([Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could)). | Keep 5–8 head-to-head pages. Fact-check and date them, and say where each rival is stronger. |
| 4 | **Video** | Aleyda Solis's August 2026 study found YouTube to be the largest single source site for SaaS brands in AI answers ([Aleyda Solis](https://www.aleydasolis.com/en/ai-search/ai-search-citations/)). Ahrefs found YouTube mentions are the strongest *correlate* of AI visibility. That is a link in the data, not proof that one causes the other ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)). Perplexity never cited YouTube in our tests, so the payoff is likely in Google's AI features and ChatGPT, which we could not test. | One trade per video, filmed on real residential plans. Customer jobs. "Quotr" said out loud and written in titles. |
| 5 | **Proof pages** | Perplexity currently calls Quotr's claims "vendor assertions." | Case studies with numbers and named customers, a page listing integrations by name, a security page, and a public accuracy test. |

**Do less of:** more self-ranked "best X" lists, more "[trade] estimating services" or city-by-city variations, and short generic definitions. Google warns that making separate pages for every variation of a question, mainly to game search or AI answers, can break its spam rules on mass-produced content ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features); [Google guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

**Where the top-of-funnel white space is:** factory-direct buying, tariffs and landed costs, multifamily cost benchmarks, and residential rebuild costs. In our tests, almost no software company was cited on these topics. None combined estimating software with factory-direct buying, as Quotr does. Answers came mostly from sourcing agents, logistics firms, factory-direct suppliers (such as Port2Site and Build Source), government reports, cost-data publishers and local builders.

The two software exceptions, Meltplan and Exayard, show that a small vendor can break in with cost data. Quotr offers an LA fire-rebuild cost estimate as one of its sample deliverables ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/service/)). Yet when we asked what an LA rebuild costs per square foot, the AI cited Bloomberg and local contractors, not Quotr.

Quotr already has the raw material for this content. It says its service has estimated over $1.2 billion of projects ([Quotr blog](https://quotr.ai/blog/)). Its procurement page lists real project prices, such as a Saratoga job at $97,000 against a $187K–$218K local market price ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/procurement/)). Today these numbers appear only as claims, with no published method or dataset behind them. The biggest content opportunity in this audit is turning that data into a dated dataset, with a clear method and sources, published under Quotr's name.

### Existing vs new content: fix what AI already reads, then build unique assets

**The short answer: fix first, because some old pages are actively hurting Quotr. Then put most new effort into unique assets, not constant refreshing.**

**Fix first.** The fastest win in this whole audit is correcting the old pricing across about 13 URLs and the llms.txt file. Then ask Google and Bing to re-read the corrected pages, and ask Nomic and Octopus Builds to update their lists. Correct every other conflicting fact the same way. Merge the duplicate Togal posts and the overlapping "estimating services" posts, so AI finds one clear version of each. Finally, fact-check the competitor details in the comparison posts, because AI uses those posts as sources.

**Make Quotr's name part of the fact.** The outsourced-estimating example shows AI will take a number and drop the brand. Write key sentences so the name travels with the fact, for example: "Quotr.ai's Estimation Service charges $0.25 per sq ft for projects under 50,000 sq ft." Start with the pages AI already fetches: the posts on outsourcing, commercial estimating services, AI takeoff accuracy and PlanSwift alternatives.

**Then build new; don't just repaint.** Freshness matters, but less than many GEO guides claim. Ahrefs found that AI-cited pages are fresher than ordinary search results, especially in ChatGPT. Yet the cited pages still average **2.9 years old**. The study's author suspects that most brands will "see better results from creating new, high-quality content than from … extremely frequent content updating" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)).

Changing a date without changing the content can backfire. Posts "lightly refreshed with '2026' in the title" were among the pages that lost Google visibility in early 2026 ([Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/is-google-finally-cracking-down-on)). Update pages when facts really change (pricing, comparisons, benchmarks), and say what changed.

A simple rule for Quotr's team: **every new page must contain something no competitor or AI could write without Quotr's data, customers or people.**

### Signals: proof from others now beats on-page tricks

| Signal | Strength of evidence | What the evidence says | What it means for Quotr |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mentions and reviews on other sites** | Strong, and the most important | For SaaS brands, **82.3%** of the top cited sources in AI Mode, Gemini and ChatGPT were other people's sites. | **Brand mentions more than links** | Strong correlation (a link in the data, not proven cause) | In Ahrefs' 75,000-brand study, YouTube mentions and brand mentions around the web tracked AI visibility more closely than link-based site authority did ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/)). | Get Quotr *talked about*, not just linked to. |
| **Normal search visibility** | Strong; still the foundation | Google says a page only needs to be indexed and eligible to appear in Search. In 2026, 38% of pages cited in Google's AI Overviews ranked in the top 10 for the same search, so a top-10 ranking helps but is not required ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)). | Good SEO still feeds AI. Growth Memo found that dates and numbers are the details that best predict ChatGPT citations ([Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/why-proprietary-data-is-your-most)). | Publish dated figures from Quotr's own work. |
| **Being named, not just footnoted** | Rising | Since May 7, 2026, ChatGPT links brand names straight to their homepages. Profound, a vendor, says B2B software gained the most, with daily ChatGPT referrals up more than 200% ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-referrals-branded-links)). OpenAI can change this feature at any time. | Each mention of Quotr's name is now a clickable doorway. The homepage must convert first-time visitors. |
| **Consistent facts everywhere** | Our inference, supported by the tests | Perplexity hedged on Quotr's factory count and funding because the sources disagreed. | One fact sheet, used on every page and profile. |
| **Real freshness** | Moderate; varies by engine | ChatGPT prefers newer pages. Google's AI Overviews care much less ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)). | Refresh pricing and comparison pages when facts change. |
| llms.txt files | Weak | Google treats these files like any other file. Common Crawl found that some llms.txt files contain prompt injections, meaning hidden attempts to give AI orders ([Common Crawl](https://commoncrawl.org/blog/a-content-analysis-of-llms-txt-files-from-the-july-2026-crawl-archive)). | Keep the file short and accurate, or drop it. |
| Special schema for AI | Weak | Google says no special markup is needed. Microsoft's October 2025 guidance recommended it; it has published newer guidance since. | Basic housekeeping only. |
| GEO "tricks" (packing in stats and quotes) | Weak | C-SEO Bench found them largely ineffective, and gains shrink as everyone copies them ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097)). | Use real data because it helps readers, not as a trick. |
| Betting on one platform | Risky | Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations fell from 3.83% to 0.52% within weeks in August 2026 ([Promptwatch](https://promptwatch.com/blog/chatgpt-stop-citing-reddit)). Another study found that, on average, only about a third of cited pages were still cited 28 days later ([Digital Authority Partners](https://www.digitalauthority.me/resources/ai-visibility-study/)). | Spread presence across many channels, and re-test monthly. |

Two more data points are useful, with caveats.

- **G2's own survey** says **51%** of B2B software buyers now start their research in an AI chatbot, and **45%** trust review-site citations most ([G2](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-research-the-answer-economy)). G2 sells reviews, so treat that as directional. Our tests point the same way: review sites fed 9 of 32 answers. But other data cuts the other way: in Aleyda Solis's SaaS data, news and review sites were only about 10% of top sources, far behind community and video sites.
- **Muck Rack**, a PR software company, reports that "earned" sources make up 84% of AI citations, and journalism alone about 27% ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026)). Muck Rack does not spell out exactly what counts as "earned", and this does not mean 84% of citations are press coverage.

## Top-of-funnel reach now means being named, not just clicked

The meeting brief asks how to keep and grow top-of-funnel reach. The honest answer: AI answers will keep taking clicks away from educational content. So reach has to be measured as **presence in AI answers**, not only as website visits.

The evidence on lost clicks is now much stronger:
- **The first randomized experiment** on this was published in August 2026 as a preprint (a study not yet peer-reviewed). It tracked about 1,100 Google users. Moving people to Google's AI Mode cut their clicks to outside websites by **18.8 percentage points**. Removing AI Overviews *raised* clicks by 8.8 points ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18352v1)).
- **SparkToro** reports that **68%** of US Google searches ended without any click in early 2026 ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/)).
- **These AI features are huge.** Google says AI Mode passed **1 billion** monthly users and AI Overviews **2.5 billion** in May 2026 ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)).

Being cited helps, but it does not undo the loss. Seer found that pages cited inside an AI Overview got 120% more clicks per view than uncited pages. But they still got 38% fewer clicks than pages on searches with no AI Overview at all. Seer also notes that this is a correlation, not proof of cause ([Seer](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)).

Whether AI visitors turn into customers is still unclear. The only peer-reviewed study looked at 973 online stores. ChatGPT visitors bought less often than visitors from every traditional channel except paid social, but did better for complex products ([Marketing Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2025.0489)). Construction software is a complex purchase, so the picture may be kinder to Quotr. That is untested, though. Quotr should measure it rather than assume it.

Quotr can grow reach in three ways. First, **show up in answers on the unclaimed topics** listed above: factory-direct buying, tariffs, multifamily costs and rebuild costs. Second, **build assets people still click through to**, such as tools, datasets and videos. Third, **be present where AI reads beyond quotr.ai**: review sites, lists, YouTube and communities.

Free tools now make this measurable:
- **Google Search Console** (Google's free dashboard for site owners) shows how often each page appears in AI Overviews and AI Mode. It has done so worldwide since August 31, 2026, though it does not show clicks ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-console-ai-reports-rolled-out-worldwide/587836/)). Google also added a way for sites to opt out of these AI features; Quotr should **not** use it.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** (Microsoft's equivalent dashboard) shows how often pages are cited in Copilot. The report is still a preview ([Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)).
- **Google Analytics (GA4)** added an "AI Assistant" traffic channel in May 2026. It does not count Perplexity, so Quotr should add a custom rule for it ([Google](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9164320)).
- **Demo and trial forms** should ask "How did you hear about us?"
- **A monthly AI test.** Quotr should re-run a fixed set of about 50 buyer questions every month in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and Copilot, because a single test can mislead.

A simple top-of-funnel scorecard would track six numbers: the share of AI answers that name Quotr, AI impressions in Search Console, branded search volume, review count, YouTube views, and demos that came from AI.

## Conclusion

Quotr does not have a content problem. It has a credibility gap. It has built a content engine that AI engines already read and trust for facts. But they pass those facts along without the brand, and they fill the gaps with errors Quotr itself published.

So the next gains will come from making Quotr's claims consistent, credited to Quotr by name and confirmed by others, not from writing more pages. That shift is also cheaper than what the team has been doing: a fact sheet, a pricing clean-up and a review drive cost days, not months.

The larger opportunity is strategic. The topics where almost no software rival appears in AI answers (factory-direct prices, tariffs and landed costs, multifamily and rebuild cost benchmarks) are the same topics where Quotr holds data no rival has. Top-of-funnel growth therefore depends less on chasing "best AI takeoff software" lists, which 15-plus vendors are already crowding. It depends more on becoming the named source for real construction cost numbers.

The first proof point should come quickly. If the 30-day clean-up works, branded answers should start quoting $79.90 once AI engines re-read the fixed pages. The borrowed "Quotr Pro" ratings will fade only as real Quotr reviews appear.
