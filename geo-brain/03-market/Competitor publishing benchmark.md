# Competitor publishing benchmark

> [!abstract] What this page is for
> This page compares how Quotr publishes with how ten rival vendors publish: blog size, rhythm, content types, years in titles, bylines, and whether they write about Quotr. It says plainly what we know and what we could not measure. Use it to set fair targets for the engagement, and to finish the count once web access is back.

> [!info]- Sources
> - **Vault notes:** the competitor profiles [[Togal AI]], [[STACK]], [[Beam AI]], [[Kreo]], [[PlanSwift]], [[Bluebeam]], [[Buildxact]], [[Handoff]] and [[AI-native newcomers]]; [[Competitor landscape]]; [[Website audit]]; [[Entity fact sheet]]; [[AI visibility baseline]]; [[Publishing beyond the blog]]; the prompt notes [[L-030 what is a construction takeoff|L-030]] and [[E-056 free construction takeoff software|E-056]]; and the research notes of 2026-09-25: [[competitor_geo_benchmark]], [[quotr_ai_visibility_tests]], [[quotr_onsite_content_audit]], [[quotr_offsite_presence]] and [[verification_quotr_and_competitors]].
> - **Data files:** the competitor part of the publishing landscape research of 2026-09-26. It has two parts: the four AI-takeoff rivals (Togal.AI, STACK, Beam AI, Kreo), and six incumbents and newcomers (PlanSwift/ConstructConnect, Bluebeam, Buildxact, Handoff, Bobyard, xBuild). Each part had its own fact-check, and this page uses the corrected values. Also the Quotr blog statistics of 2026-09-26 (96 posts, dated by the blog sitemap) and the blog post ID list of 2026-09-26.
> - **How fresh this is:** no new web search ran for this page. The shared search limit was used up (200 of 200), and page downloads are blocked. So every competitor fact comes from the vault research of 2026-09-25. The fact-check on 2026-09-26 compared that research with the vault only, not with the live web. This page uses no Google facts.

> [!note] Words used on this page
> - **Sitemap:** the list of pages a site gives to search engines. Each entry can carry a **lastmod** date (the "last changed" date), which we use as a stand-in for the publish date.
> - **Slug:** the last part of a web address, for example `best-togal-ai-alternatives-2026`.
> - **Byline:** the "By ..." line that names the writer of a post.
> - **Comparison hub:** a separate section of a site that holds all the "us vs them" pages, for example `ibeam.ai/compare`.
> - **Wire release:** a press release sent through a paid newswire, which re-posts it on many news sites.
> - **Cited:** an AI answer used the page as a source link. **Named:** the AI answer mentioned the brand in its text.
> - **Seen:** the URL is recorded in the vault research of 2026-09-25. A "seen" count is a floor, not an estimate of blog size.
> - **Site search:** a web search limited to one site (for example, Quotr on handoff.ai), run on 2026-09-25. "None found" by site search is a strong hint, not proof.
> - **Found in web search:** seen with our web search tool. The tool is not Google.

## The short version

- **We could not count competitor posts.** Blog size and monthly rhythm are unknown for all ten rivals. The search limit ran out and page downloads are blocked. We only have 0 to 9 sample blog URLs per rival from the 2026-09-25 research.
- **Quotr's own rhythm is on record.** The blog has 96 posts. It put out 22 in May and 25 in June 2026, then 17 in July, 11 in August and 7 in September (to the 25th). The only rival rhythm we saw is Kreo's: 3 product posts between 25 August and 17 September 2026. That is too few to call a monthly rate.
- **Rivals get named; Quotr gets cited.** In 32 Perplexity questions that named no vendor (2026-09-25), ibeam.ai and stackct.com were each cited in 7, and kreo.net and quotr.ai in 6 each. When a rival's site was cited, the rival was usually named too (12 of 19 recorded cases). Quotr was named in 1 of its 6.
- **Rivals publish more outside their blogs.** They use wire releases, webinars, podcasts, a conference, award posts and review profiles. Quotr's news mostly stays on its own blog.
- **Quotr does things rivals do not.** It has 9 posts about 5 rivals by name. No rival site we checked mentions Quotr. 4 of Quotr's 5 "alternatives" posts were cited by Perplexity.
- **Years in titles.** 27 of Quotr's 96 post URLs carry a year. In the rival URLs we saw, a year appears mainly on news items and a few flagship guides.
- **Next step:** read each rival's blog sitemap once web access is back. The steps are in [[#How to finish this benchmark]].

### What both sides do

- **Dated company news.** Kreo and STACK post product news. Quotr posts news too: the new pricing post ([[BP-93 new pricing|BP-93]], 14 Sep 2026), a "$1.2B+" milestone post (24 Sep 2026) and 6 event recaps.
- **A facts page for AI assistants.** Handoff's "official information" page (updated September 2026) and Quotr's /disambiguation/ page do the same job. Both need fixing. Handoff's gives its prices per year instead of per month. Quotr's carries facts that other sources contradict, such as the headquarters city and founding year (task [[A5 Rewrite disambiguation as a plain company facts page|A5]]).
- **A glossary.** Quotr has 55 dictionary terms; Kreo has an A-to-Z glossary.
- **Trade pages.** Quotr has 23 trade pages. Beam has 15, Togal at least 8, Kreo 8. Only one Quotr trade page (drywall) was opened; it had about 25 unique words. The other 22 were not checked.

### A working idea, not a finding

Rivals may be cited for a few strong, updated flagship pages rather than for how much they publish. Examples: Handoff's 2026 list, Bluebeam's 2026 takeoffs guide and ConstructConnect's 2026 guide. Quotr's 22-25 posts a month in May-June 2026 did not win the category questions (Quotr was named in 0 of 15). But rival volumes are unknown, and buildxact.com was cited in 7-8 questions through several different pages. So treat this as an idea to test, not a rule.

## How to finish this benchmark

You need web access for this: a fresh web search budget and page downloads allowed. A person can also do it by hand in a browser. Do Quotr on the same day with the same steps, so both sides are measured the same way.

### Steps

1. **Find each blog's sitemap.** Open `https://<domain>/robots.txt` and look for lines that start with "Sitemap:". If there are none, try `/sitemap.xml` and `/sitemap_index.xml`.
2. **Open the blog part of the sitemap.** Count only URLs under the blog path in the table below. Leave out category, tag and author pages.
3. **Copy each URL's lastmod date.** Count posts per month for January to September 2026. Lastmod means "last changed", not "published". Watch for many posts sharing one date: that is a bulk reset, as happened to 6 Quotr posts.
4. **Check the dates by hand.** Open the 10 newest posts and 5 random older ones. Write down the date shown on the page and the byline. If the page dates often differ from lastmod, use the page dates.
5. **If there is no sitemap,** look for an RSS feed (a machine-readable list of the newest posts), or page through the blog index and count. Some blog index pages load posts with JavaScript (Quotr's shows "Showing 12 of 96 posts"), so a plain download may show only the first page.
6. **Sort the 20 newest posts by type,** using the same groups as the [[Blog health audit]]: best-of list, buyer's guide, how-to, explainer, comparison, news, customer story, data report.
7. **Count the year-stamped URLs:** the share of all blog URLs with "2025" or "2026" in the slug.
8. **Count comparison pages outside the blog,** using the paths in the table below.
9. **Search each site for "Quotr".** Do the four never checked first: Bluebeam, PlanSwift, Bobyard and xBuild. Find xBuild's domain before you start.
10. **Record and log.** Fill the recording table, update the at-a-glance table, and set `last_verified`. Add a line to [[Changelog]]. Save the raw URL lists as a new dated research note.
