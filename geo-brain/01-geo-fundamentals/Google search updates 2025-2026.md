# Google search updates 2025-2026

> [!abstract] What this page is for
> A plain-English record of what Google changed between January 2025 and 2026-09-26: ranking updates, Search Console, AI Overviews and AI Mode, and the spam and quality rules. ## The short version

- **Google has been busy.** From January 2025 to today it ran five broad core updates (big changes to how Google ranks pages), one core update for Discover only (Discover is Google's feed of suggested articles) and five spam updates (changes aimed at pages that break Google's spam rules). See RANK-01 to RANK-21.
- **A spam update is running right now.** It started on 2026-09-24 and may last until about 2026-10-08. 95 of Quotr's 96 posts were live when it began (RANK-21).
- **Most of the blog has never faced a core update.** Only 21 of 96 posts were live when the May 2026 core update began. Only 1 of the 22 best-of, buyer's-guide and alternatives posts was live. No core update has started since (RANK-13, RANK-22).
- **Quotr's self-ranked lists fit a risky pattern.** SaaS (subscription software) and B2B (business-to-business) sites that rank themselves first in their own "best X" lists lost 29-49% of Google visibility in early 2026, and the losses grew in May. This is an analyst's observation. Google has not confirmed it, and other analysts dispute the cause (RANK-07, RANK-08, RANK-15, QUALITY-12).
- **Google says AI answers need no tricks.** AI Overviews (the AI summary at the top of Google results) and AI Mode (Google's chat-style search) only need a post that is indexed (stored by Google so it can appear in results) and allowed to show a snippet (the short text under a search result). The main factor is "valuable, unique, non-commodity content". A page for every variation of a question can count as spam (INDEX-05, AI-08, QUALITY-05).
- **Search Console now shows which posts appear in AI answers.** Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how a site does in Google Search. Since 2026-08-31 it shows AI Overview and AI Mode impressions (times a page was shown) per page, for every site. It shows impressions only, from 2026-05-18. Older numbers need care: impressions were over-counted until 2026-04-27 (GSC-12, GSC-14, GSC-16).
- **We have not seen Quotr's Search Console yet.** So we do not know whether any post was actually hit. Also, no Google fact on this page was re-checked by a second search (see [[#How sure are we?]]). The first job is the [[Search Console audit playbook]].

## How sure are we?

> [!warning] Read this before you quote a Google fact to Quotr
> - **Searched live today.** The 42 claims about ranking updates (RANK) and Search Console (GSC) were found by live web search on 2026-09-26. None was re-checked by a second, independent search, because the search limit ran out.
> - **Carried over.** The 93 claims about indexing (INDEX), AI Overviews and AI Mode (AI), quality rules (QUALITY), freshness (FRESH) and other engines (OTHER) come from the vault research of 2026-09-25. That research used search summaries, Perplexity answers and some full-page reads. It was fact-checked only where the vault's fact-check notes say so (marked **FC** in the [[#Claim register]]). None was searched again today.
> - **Ratings.** The research rated 32 claims high, 82 medium and 21 low confidence. Low usually means one secondary source.
> - **Weakest spots.** Some facts rest on industry press only: the dates of the March 2025, December 2025 and March 2026 core updates and the August 2026 spam update, the num=100 change, both Page indexing report freezes, and the FAQ rich-result removal (one article).
> - **Not covered at all.** Changes to Googlebot (Google's page-reading bot), JavaScript rendering (how Google reads pages built by scripts), guidance on sitemaps (the list of pages a site gives to search engines) and lastmod (the "last changed" date in a sitemap), canonical handling (which version of a page counts as the main one), de-indexing waves, Quality Rater Guidelines updates (Google's guide for its human raters) and site reputation abuse (a Google spam policy). Treat these as unknown, not as "no change".
> - **Inferences are labelled.** For example: the guess about when the next core update comes, the advice to hold bulk changes during an update, and which posts are "most exposed".
> - **No Quotr data yet.** "Live during an update" only means a post could have been affected.
>

## AI Overviews and AI Mode

- **Reach.** At Google I/O on 2026-05-19, Google said AI Mode had passed 1 billion monthly users and AI Overviews 2.5 billion (AI-03, [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)). Gemini 3.5 Flash became AI Mode's default model (AI-02). A follow-up question on an AI Overview now continues inside AI Mode (AI-04). AI Overviews reportedly moved to Gemini 3 in January 2026 (AI-01; one source, low confidence).
- **Google's rules.** There are no extra requirements: a page must be indexed and allowed to show a snippet (AI-05, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). The May 2026 guide says llms.txt, Markdown copies, special schema and chunking are not needed, and names "valuable, unique, non-commodity content" as the most important factor (AI-08, [Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing)).
- **Query fan-out.** Both surfaces may run several related searches to build one answer (AI-06). Making separate content for every such variation can count as scaled content abuse (AI-07).
- **Preferred Sources.** From 2026-05-27 a user's favourite sites get a badge in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google says people are twice as likely to click a Preferred Source, and more than 345,000 sources had been chosen (AI-09, [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/original-high-quality-content-search/)).
- **Opt-out rules may change.** The UK competition regulator (CMA) wants page-level AI controls by March 2027 (AI-11).
- **Which queries get AI answers.** Seer: 95.4% of "X vs Y", 81.3% of "best of" and 83.4% of price or cost queries. Only 36% of informational, 8% of commercial and 5% of transactional queries (AI-17, [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)).
- **Citations are not just the top 10.** Ahrefs: 38% of AI Overview citations also ranked top 10 in 2026, against 76% in July 2025. Part of the fall comes from better detection in Ahrefs' own tools. BrightEdge estimated about 17% with a different method (AI-18, [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)).
- **Cited is not recommended.** When a brand's own "best X" list was cited in an AI Overview, the brand was left out of the recommendation 69% of the time (AI-19, [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/why-calling-yourself-the-best-could)).
- **Third-party sources lead for SaaS.** 82.3% of SaaS citations were third-party. In AI Mode, social and community sources were 74.6% of SaaS top-cited sources. YouTube was the largest SaaS source, and Reddit appeared in all 15 SaaS panels (AI-20, [Aleyda Solis](https://www.aleydasolis.com/en/ai-search/ai-search-citations/)).

## Spam and quality rules

- **Scaled content abuse.** Google's spam policies list "using generative AI tools to generate many pages without adding value" (QUALITY-01) and "creating many pages with search keywords that make little sense to readers" (QUALITY-02). Both quotes come from the vault's notes; the policy page was not re-read today ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)).
- **Pages for every question variation.** The May 2026 guide says separate content for every fan-out variation, made mainly to steer AI answers, breaks that policy. Genuine subtopic coverage is fine: "focus on what your users want, and avoid overdoing it" (QUALITY-05, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).
- **Keyword stuffing** (filling a page with keywords to manipulate rankings) is a violation. Credit to Quotr: the vault audit found no classic keyword stuffing on the site today (QUALITY-03).
- **The main positive factor** is "valuable, unique, non-commodity content" (QUALITY-06). AI features only use pages that are indexed and can show a snippet (QUALITY-07).
- **Who, how and why.** Google's helpful-content guidance asks that it is clear who made the content. It encourages accurate bylines that lead to author information. It also asks creators to explain how content was made, including AI use where readers would expect to know, and why it exists (QUALITY-08, QUALITY-09, [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).
- **AI writing itself is not the problem.** Ahrefs (600,000 pages) found AI-generated content does not hurt rankings (vendor study, correlation only) (QUALITY-15). In another Ahrefs study, 25.8% of top-3 cited links in AI Overviews were "pure human" and 71.7% mixed AI and human writing (QUALITY-16).
- **Self-promotional lists.** Named per-site losses were 49%, 43%, 42%, 38%, 34% and 29% (QUALITY-10). The hardest-hit patterns were mass-produced "best X" lists, self-promotional comparison pages, scaled "alternatives" pages, spammy structured data and posts lightly re-dated with "2026" (QUALITY-11). Google has not confirmed a targeted update (QUALITY-12).
- **Year labels pull both ways.** A vendor benchmark with no published method says about 3 in 4 B2B SaaS pages cited by ChatGPT had a year in the title (QUALITY-17). Our advice (not a Google rule): keep "2026" only when prices, entries and facts were really re-checked for 2026.
- **Not covered.** Quality Rater Guidelines updates, site reputation abuse, expired domain abuse and manual-action trends in 2025-2026 are unknown.

## Freshness and refreshing

- **AI tools cite slightly fresher pages.** Ahrefs (2025-07-28; about 17 million cited URLs): pages cited by AI assistants averaged 1,064 days old, against 1,432 days for Google organic results, 25.7% fresher (FRESH-01, [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)).
- **ChatGPT cares most, AI Overviews least.** In the same study, ChatGPT leaned most toward recent pages. AI Overview citations were about as old as organic results (FRESH-02). Ahrefs contradicts itself on the 1,432 figure, so quote it with care (FRESH-03).
- **Age is not Quotr's main problem.** AI-cited pages still averaged about 2.9 years old (FRESH-04). By blog-sitemap dates, 94 of Quotr's 96 posts are under six months old. Inference: quality and duplicates matter more than age.
- **New content beats constant tinkering.** The study's author suspects most brands do better creating new, high-quality content than updating very often (FRESH-05).
- **Date-only changes look risky.** Google's John Mueller reportedly warns against changing only the date (second-hand, via Ahrefs) (FRESH-06). Posts lightly re-dated with "2026" were among the early-2026 losers (FRESH-07, FRESH-08). Google has not confirmed a targeted update (FRESH-09).
- **Two numbers to avoid.** "Half of AI citations come from content updated in the last 13 weeks" and "Perplexity cites content updated within 30 days up to 142% more" have no known original dataset (FRESH-10). Do not use them to sell a refresh schedule.
- **Lists still get cited by ChatGPT.** In an Ahrefs study (date unknown), "best X" lists were 43.8% of the page types ChatGPT cited (FRESH-11). Inference: a few honest, maintained lists are safer than many self-ranked ones.
- **A test for a real update.** A refresh should add something new that only Quotr has: its own prices, test results, customer numbers or trade detail (FRESH-12). Growth Memo found dates and numbers are the details that best predict ChatGPT citations (FRESH-14, low confidence).
- **Measure it.** The Generative AI reports let you compare AI impressions per post before and after each refresh or merge (FRESH-13).
- **Quotr's dates today.** On 2026-09-25 the main sitemap showed the fetch day as every URL's lastmod, and the 96-post blog sitemap was not listed in robots.txt (FRESH-15). A second fetch is needed to prove lastmod always equals the fetch day. The two Togal alternatives posts ([[BP-32 best togal ai alternatives|BP-32]] and [[BP-43 best togal ai alternatives 2026|BP-43]]) are near-duplicates, and both were cited in the vault's AI tests (FRESH-16).
- **Not covered.** Google's guidance on visible dates and dateModified, what Google counts as a meaningful update, pruning experiments, merge-plus-redirect versus canonical, and how to handle "-2026" URLs at year end.
