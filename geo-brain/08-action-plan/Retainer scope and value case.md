## The short version

- **Quotr built fast, and part of it works.** It published 96 blog posts in about six months, peaking at 25 in June 2026. Perplexity, the one AI engine tested, reads the pages: quotr.ai was in the sources of 6 of 32 unbranded answers (questions that do not name Quotr). But Quotr was named in only 1 of those 32.
- **Some pages now work against Quotr.** About 13 posts (possibly 16) and llms.txt (a summary file for AI tools) still showed retired prices 11 days after the 14 September price change. In 2 of 8 brand questions, Perplexity told buyers Quotr starts at about $299.90 a month, about 3.75 times the real $79.90 Lite price.
- **The Google risk lies ahead.** Quotr has 22 "best" and "alternatives" lists. Quotr ranks itself first in the 4 we checked. Practitioners reported that sites with self-ranked lists lost 29–49% of their Google visibility in 2026. Google has not confirmed this. Only 21 of 96 posts were live when the last core update (a broad change to how Google ranks pages) began.
- **The blog needs tidying, not growth.** 20 groups of posts compete for the same buyer question (59 posts, 16 proposed merges). 15 of 36 checked posts did not come back in targeted web searches. That tool is not Google, so this is a lead to check, not proof.
- **Proof from other websites is thin.** We found about 20 items outside quotr.ai in 20 months, no independent "best of" list naming Quotr and reportedly 0 G2 reviews. Since 2026-08-31, Google Search Console (Google's free site report) shows, page by page, how often each page appeared in Google's AI answers (AI Overviews and AI Mode), for every site. So part of the work can now be measured with Google's own data (impressions only, no clicks).
- **What the engagement delivers:** 12 months of work in seven workstreams: fix and refresh the blog, measure, track AI answers, build proof, earn mentions on other sites, keep the site technically clean, and report monthly. Draft price: from $8,500 a month ($102,000 a year).
- **What it cannot do:** guarantee rankings, AI mentions or traffic. AI engines and Google change often. The audit had no access to Quotr's own data.

## What we would do

Seven workstreams. Each one builds on tasks and playbooks already in the vault. Quotr's team keeps ownership of most tasks, as set out in the [[30-60-90 plan]]. The consultant plans, briefs, checks, measures and reports. In the Growth package, the consultant also drafts pages and runs outreach.

| # | Workstream | What it fixes | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fix and refresh the blog | Wrong prices and facts, duplicate posts, weak lists | Monthly batches |
| 2 | Measurement and Search Console audits | No data on indexing, clicks or AI impressions | One-off setup in October, then monthly, plus after every Google update |
| 3 | AI visibility tracking across engines | Only one engine (Perplexity) tested so far | Monthly; all six engines quarterly |
| 4 | Original data and proof content | Few facts that only Quotr can publish | Quarterly (Core) or monthly (Growth) |
| 5 | Off-site presence (reviews, lists, press) | Almost no proof on other people's sites | Monthly outreach batches; reviews checked weekly |
| 6 | Technical hygiene | Crawl paths, dates, staging copies, llms.txt | One batch in October, then a monthly check |

## Value case

> [!important] How to read this section
> This is a simple "what would need to be true" check, not a forecast. It uses only prices from the [[Entity fact sheet]]. Everything else is a labelled **assumption** for the consultant to replace with Quotr's own numbers ([[Q-31 Win-loss|Q-31]], [[Q-40 Access and history|Q-40]]). All sums are revenue unless marked as profit. No results are promised.

### Facts used (from the Entity fact sheet)

| Fact | Value | Confidence in the fact sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Software, Lite plan | $79.90 per seat per month | High |
| Software, Plus plan | $299.90 per seat per month | High |
| Software, Enterprise plan | Custom pricing | High |
| Estimation Service | $0.25 per sq ft under 50,000 sq ft; $0.10 per sq ft for 50,000+ sq ft | High |
| One procurement example (Myren Dr, Saratoga) | $97,000, against a $187K–$218K Bay Area market price ("Quotr reports") | Medium |

### Step 1: what one sale is worth to Quotr (revenue)

| Sale | Calculation | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Plus seat for 12 months | $299.90 × 12 | $3,598.80 |
| 1 Lite seat for 12 months | $79.90 × 12 | $958.80 |
| 1 Service estimate, 20,000 sq ft | 20,000 × $0.25 | $5,000 |
| 1 Service estimate, 40,000 sq ft | 40,000 × $0.25 | $10,000 |

The project sizes are examples, not Quotr data. Both stay under 50,000 sq ft, so the $0.25 rate applies. Seat revenue uses the monthly list price. Annual pricing is not shown on /pricing/ (TO CONFIRM with Quotr); a yearly discount would lower these figures. Revenue is counted over each customer's first 12 months, even if part of it falls after the engagement year.

### Step 2: how many sales would cover the fee (revenue only)

| To cover… | Core ($102,000) | Growth ($132,000) |
|---|---|---|
| Plus seats kept for 12 months | 29 | 37 |
| Lite seats kept for 12 months | 107 | 138 |
| Service estimates of 20,000 sq ft | 21 | 27 |
| Service estimates of 40,000 sq ft | 11 | 14 |

**A mixed example (all assumptions).** For Core, one mix that covers the fee in revenue:
- 5 new customers with 2 Plus seats each for a year: $35,988.
- 8 Service estimates of 30,000 sq ft ($7,500 each): $60,000.
- 10 Lite seats for a year: $9,588.
- **Total: $105,576**, just above $102,000.

**Procurement is left out of the sum.** Quotr reports one procurement project (Saratoga) at $97,000. But Quotr's margin on an order is not public. If Quotr shares it, procurement may change the picture a lot. Ask.

### Step 3: what would need to be true (worked example)

| Assumption (to replace with Quotr's data) | Value used | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Assumption 1.** An average new software customer buys 2 Plus seats | 2 seats = $7,197.60 a year | Core needs **15 new customers** in a year (about 1.25 a month). Growth needs **19** |
| **Assumption 2.** Customers stay 24 months, not 12 | $14,395.20 per customer over 2 years | Core needs **8 new customers** |
| **Assumption 3.** Quotr keeps 70% of software revenue as gross profit (a placeholder, not a Quotr figure) | $5,038.32 of profit per 2-seat customer in 12 months | Core needs **21 new customers** to cover the fee from profit |
| **Assumption 4.** 1 in 5 demos becomes a customer (a placeholder) | 15 customers ÷ 20% | About **75 extra demos a year**, about 6 a month |
| **Assumption 5.** These customers would not have come anyway | Counted with the "How did you hear about us?" field, GA4 and CRM (sales database) tags | Agree the counting rule at kickoff ([[KPIs and dashboard]], G1–G5) |

**In plain words:** with these assumptions, the Core package pays for itself if the work helps bring in roughly 15–21 new two-seat Plus customers a year (fewer if customers stay longer), or an equal mix of seats and Service jobs. Whether that is realistic depends on Quotr's real demo numbers, conversion rate, seats per customer, how long customers stay and margins. We do not have those yet.

### Value we cannot put a number on (yet)

- **Stopping a known leak.** In 2 of 8 brand questions, buyers who asked AI to compare Quotr with rivals were told an entry price about 3.75 times the real one (W-01). We cannot measure how many buyers dropped out at that point.
- **Protecting the lists before the next core update.** 22 lists match a reported risk pattern. Reworking them should lower that risk (inference). No one can promise it prevents a loss.
- **Making results visible.** Search Console's AI report, Bing and GA4 give monthly numbers in place of guesswork. Limits: Google's AI report shows impressions only, and a small site may show little data.
- **Consistent facts.** When Quotr's own pages disagree, AI engines hedge ("vendor assertions"). One fact sheet used everywhere removes one cause of that. The lack of outside proof is the other.

| When | What | Time from Quotr |

| Monthly (first week) | AI test run, Search Console and Bing exports, dashboard, written report and a call | 45–60 minutes for the call |

| Section | What it shows | KPIs |

| AI presence, one row per engine | How often Quotr is named, pages cited, "cited, not named" turned into named, share of voice (Quotr's share of all brand names mentioned), brand-answer errors | L2–L6 |
| Search Console and Bing | Clicks and AI impressions per post, indexing problems, Bing AI citations, Google updates marked on the chart | L2, G3 |

| Facts | Pages still showing retired or conflicting facts | L9 |

**Rules we will follow when reading the numbers** ([[KPIs and dashboard]]):
- Report each engine on its own line. Never blend them.
- Judge 3-month trends, not single months. Count a target as met only if it holds two months in a row.
- With 32 questions, one question is about 3 percentage points. So a one-question change is within normal noise.
- Do not compare Search Console impressions across 2026-04-27 (an impressions bug was fixed then). Use clicks for older periods.

## Risks and honest limits

**What we cannot promise**

| Limit | Why |
|---|---|
| Rankings, AI mentions or traffic | Google and each AI engine decide what to show. We can improve the inputs, not the outcome |
| Stable AI answers | A vendor (Profound) reports that 40–60% of cited domains change from month to month for the same question. One engine's behaviour can change within weeks |
| Protection from Google updates | The September spam update is running now. Another core update is plausible soon (inference). The losses for self-ranked lists are practitioner reports that Google has not confirmed |
| More clicks | AI Overviews appear on 81.3% of "best of", 95.4% of "X vs Y" and 83.4% of price and cost searches (Seer study). Every study in the Google briefing finds fewer clicks when an AI answer appears. Google says total clicks are "relatively stable" (August 2025) but has published no data outsiders can test. Reach can grow while visits fall |
| Clean attribution | A buyer who sees Quotr in an AI answer often searches the name later. Analytics then records branded search or a direct visit, not AI |
| Results without Quotr's time | Most tasks have Quotr owners. If approvals, developer time or customer permissions stall, results stall |
| Third-party decisions | List editors, reviewers and journalists decide for themselves. We can pitch, not place |

**What this audit could not check**

| Gap | Effect |
|---|---|
| No access to Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, GA4, the CRM or Cloudflare | We do not know Quotr's traffic, which posts are indexed, their AI impressions, or whether any post was hit by a Google update |
| The web search limit was reached on 2026-09-26 | The Google claims were not re-checked against their sources. 60 posts were not checked in web search at all |
| The search tool is not Google | The 15 "not found" posts are leads for URL Inspection, not proof they are missing from Google |
| AI tests covered one engine (Perplexity) on one day (2026-09-25) | ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews, Gemini, Claude and Copilot have no baseline yet |
| Only about 6 posts were read in full | Many findings rest on search-index text, which can lag behind the live page |
| Post dates come from the blog sitemap | 6 posts carry a bulk-reset date, so their real publish dates are unknown |
| Some counts are ranges | Old price: about 13 posts confirmed, up to 16. AI-cited posts: 8 confirmed as cited in unbranded answers; 4 more were flagged in the data but were retrieved only or mis-flagged |
