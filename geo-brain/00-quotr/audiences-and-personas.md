## How to read this page

- A **persona** is a short, realistic profile of one type of buyer. It helps writers pick topics and wording.
- **"Index-only"** means a quote was seen only in a search engine's stored copy of a quotr.ai page (WebSearch, 2026-09-25), not on the live page.
- **"Targeted on site"** tells you whether Quotr's website speaks to this persona today:
  - **Yes** = there is a clear path, page or product for them.
  - **Partly** = blog posts exist, but no dedicated page or proof.
  - **Implied** = Quotr's copy mentions them, but there is little or no content for them.
- **Goals, pains, triggers, objections and "where they research" are working assumptions.** Survey numbers and AI test results are sourced; the rest is our reading. They are built from the research notes, Quotr's own pages and public surveys. They are not from Quotr customer interviews. Check them with 5–10 real customers before using them in ads or sales copy.
- **Example questions** are written the way real people type into AI tools: short, informal, often with a year or a trade. Several come straight from the prompts tested in the research (IDs like C1 or P5 point to <../../research_notes/Quotr GEO AEO strategy audit/quotr_ai_visibility_tests.md>). All tests used Perplexity only. C1, C9, C11, C12 and the branded tests were re-run and held up; V1, V3, C13 and P5 were run in one session only, so treat those results as indicative (<../../research_notes/Quotr GEO AEO strategy audit/verification_quotr_and_competitors.md>).

## 3. Persona 1 — Trade subcontractor owner / estimator

**Targeted on site: Yes.** This is Quotr Software's core buyer ("Quotr.ai runs the full subcontractor workflow — takeoff, estimates, a pricing database, bids, and proposals — in one product", index-only text of /contractors/).

**Who they are**
- Owner, estimator or project manager at a specialty trade company (electrical, HVAC, plumbing, drywall, flooring, concrete, roofing and so on).
- Usually a small or mid-size company. In smaller firms the owner often does the estimates after hours; larger subs have dedicated estimators. (Assumption; confirm with customer interviews.)
- Real example: RL Electric (Quotr case study and YouTube video).

**Goals**
- Bid more jobs without hiring another estimator.
- Get takeoffs right so they do not lose money on missed scope.
- Send clean, professional proposals fast.
- Get better material prices.

**Pains**
- Manual takeoff takes many hours (RL Electric: about 20 hours before Quotr).
- Estimators are hard to hire and expensive.
- Scope gaps and change orders eat margin (Quotr post: [Scope Gaps Cost More Than Pricing Errors](https://quotr.ai/blog/scope-gap-construction/)).
- Older desktop tools (PlanSwift, On-Screen Takeoff) feel slow; Bluebeam is not built for estimating.
- Past AI tools were "hit-or-miss" (r/estimators threads).

**Buying triggers**
- A spike in bid invitations, or a lost bid because the price was late.
- An estimator quits.
- A renewal notice from their current takeoff tool.
- A costly missed-scope job.
- Moving from a Windows desktop tool to the browser or a Mac.

**Objections**
- "AI takeoff is not accurate on my drawings." (The most common worry; data accuracy is contractors' #1 AI concern.)
- "I do not trust a small company with no reviews."
- "Will it work for my trade's symbols?"
- "I already pay for Bluebeam / PlanSwift / STACK."
- "Is my bid data secure?"

**Where they research:** Google and ChatGPT for "best [trade] estimating software"; YouTube demos; Reddit r/estimators; trade Facebook groups; peers; supplier reps; trade shows.

**What AI tools recommend today:** For "best AI takeoff software for subcontractors 2026" (test C1, run twice and reproduced), Perplexity names Togal.AI, STACK, PlanSwift and Beam AI. Quotr is not named and not cited.

## 4. Persona 2 — General contractor / preconstruction manager

**Targeted on site: Partly.** Blog posts exist (preconstruction-services, commercial-estimating-services, how-to-bid-commercial-construction-projects, scope gaps). /disambiguation/ names "commercial general contractors". The post ["Preconstruction Services for General Contractors: Precon Capacity Without the Headcount (2026)"](https://quotr.ai/blog/preconstruction-services/) pitches Quotr Service to GCs as estimating capacity on demand, against the cost of a full-time precon estimator (search-index text). There is no GC landing page, no GC case study and no bid-levelling page.

**Who they are**
- Preconstruction manager, chief estimator or project executive at a general contractor.
- Collects sub bids, builds budgets for owners and developers, and levels bids (compares sub prices line by line).

**Goals**
- Fast, reliable budgets at early design stages.
- Complete scope coverage across all trades.
- Clean bid levelling and buyout.
- Win more negotiated and hard-bid work.

**Pains**
- Precon teams are stretched; budgets are needed before drawings are complete.
- Scope gaps between subs cause change orders.
- Sub coverage is thin on some trades.
- Material price swings and tariffs make budgets go stale.

**Buying triggers**
- A pipeline spike or a big pursuit.
- A precon staff gap.
- An owner asking for faster budget updates.
- A buyout that went over budget.

**Objections**
- "We already use Procore / BuildingConnected / STACK."
- "Can it cover all trades, not just one?"
- "Security and data control for owner drawings."
- "A small vendor is a risk for an enterprise contract."

**Where they research:** Peers and industry groups (AGC), ENR and Construction Dive, G2/Capterra, vendor webinars, ChatGPT for first-pass shortlists.

**What AI tools recommend today:** For "construction bid management software for general contractors" (C7), Perplexity names ConstructConnect, BuildingConnected, SmartBid, PlanHub, Procore, Kahua and Dodge. For residential bid levelling, Buildertrend, Buildxact, SmartBid, Procore and Contractor Foreman (competitor benchmark §1). Quotr is absent.

## 5. Persona 3 — Residential builder / small GC / remodeler

**Targeted on site: Implied.** Quotr's llms.txt, /faq/ and /disambiguation/ say it "supports residential construction including single-family homes and multi-family housing". But the same /disambiguation/ page calls Quotr a platform for "commercial general contractors, large specialty subcontractors, and real estate development funds", the drywall page says "from commercial floor plans", and there is no residential hub, residential case study or residential cost content.

**Who they are**
- Owner of a homebuilding, custom-home, ADU or remodeling company, often doing estimates personally.
- Builds a few to a few dozen homes or projects a year. Buys a lot of finish materials (windows, doors, cabinets, flooring, fixtures).

**Goals**
- Price jobs quickly and accurately from plans.
- Protect margin when material prices rise.
- Look professional to homeowners and lenders.
- Save on finish materials.

**Pains**
- Tariffs and material price jumps (JEC estimates $7,500–$10,900 added per home; see competitor benchmark §3).
- Estimating takes nights and weekends.
- Subs' quotes arrive late or with gaps.
- Long lead times on windows and cabinets.

**Buying triggers**
- A new project or a jump in bid requests.
- A price shock on materials.
- A lost job because the estimate was slow.
- A rebuild wave (for example, LA fire rebuilding). Quotr has history here: it launched a free "FireTips" app for LA fire victims in February 2025 ([firetips.quotr.io](https://firetips.quotr.io/); EIN Presswire release, WebSearch 2026-09-25).

**Objections**
- "Most estimating software is built for commercial jobs."
- "I need something simple."
- "Is importing materials from China risky (quality, codes, delays)?"
- "About 90-day lead time is too long for my schedule." (Lead time from index-only /contractors/ text; **TO CONFIRM**.)

**Where they research:** Google and ChatGPT; YouTube; Facebook builder groups; NAHB/IBS and local builder associations (Quotr is a BIA Bay Area member); suppliers; Houzz.

**What AI tools recommend today:** For "best construction estimating software for residential contractors" (C2): Clear Estimates, Buildxact, Houzz Pro, Buildertrend, JobTread, QuickBooks. For "AI estimating software for residential general contractors that goes from plans to proposal" (C13): Handoff, BuildVision AI, Houzz Pro, Buildxact, Beam. Quotr is absent from both.

## 6. Persona 4 — Real estate developer (single-family, multifamily, ADU, rebuild)

**Targeted on site: Yes.** The homepage "For developers" path goes to /service/. /procurement/ shows three projects at Bay Area residential addresses. Blog posts cover pro formas, how developers source materials, and a "Developer Desk". BiltWise uses Quotr "for developer-stage budgets" (index-only). Service sample "Fast Cost Estimation (Residential LA Fire Rebuilding)".

**Who they are**
- Small to mid-size developer or owner's rep building single-family homes, small multifamily, ADUs or rebuilds.
- Needs cost numbers before buying land or getting a loan. Often has no in-house estimator.

**Goals**
- A trustworthy construction budget before committing to a deal.
- A pro forma that updates when costs change.
- Lower hard costs, especially finish materials.
- Speed: decide on deals in days, not weeks.

**Pains**
- GC budgets take weeks and may be padded.
- Pro formas "never stop changing" (Quotr blog title).
- Material cost inflation and tariffs.
- Hard to check whether bids are fair.

**Buying triggers**
- A deal under contract or a land purchase.
- A lender asking for a cost breakdown.
- A value-engineering exercise after bids come in high.
- A rebuild or entitlement milestone.

**Objections**
- "Will the estimate be accurate enough for underwriting?"
- "Who stands behind the numbers?"
- "Factory-direct materials: quality, warranty and code compliance."
- "I have never heard of Quotr." (No press, no reviews.)

**Where they research:** Peers and brokers, BiggerPockets-style communities (not checked in the research), ChatGPT and Google for cost-per-square-foot questions, industry events (Quotr has published recaps of RE:Forge SF 2026 and PCBC 2026).

## 10. Persona 8 — Estimating service buyer (outsourcing)

**Targeted on site: Yes.** About 13 "estimating services" posts published Aug–Sep 2026 (onsite audit §2), plus /service/ with sample deliverables and per-sq-ft pricing.

**Who they are:** Any sub, GC, builder or developer who wants estimates done for them: short-staffed teams, seasonal peaks, or firms without an estimator.

**Goals:** Reliable estimates on time at a known price.

**Pains:** Hiring estimators is slow; freelance quality varies; unclear pricing among estimating firms.

**Triggers:** Bid deadline pile-up; estimator leaves; a new trade or market.

**Objections:** "Will they understand my trade and region?" "Turnaround?" "Who is accountable for errors?"

**What AI tools do today:** For "outsourced construction estimating service price per square foot for developers" (C12), Perplexity's **first citation is Quotr's own post**, and it uses Quotr's $0.25/$0.10 per sq ft rates, but it attributes them to "some firms" and never names Quotr (reproduced in the verification file). Quotr's content is trusted; its brand is not attached.
