# Publishing beyond the blog

> [!abstract] What this page is for
> A dated record of what Quotr has published outside its blog: press releases, podcasts, videos, social posts, launches and profiles. It shows how often each channel is used, what is out of date, and what to do next. ## The short version

- **Very little outside the blog.** Over about 20 months (February 2025 to September 2026) we found about 20 items outside the blog: about 12 pieces of content and about 8 profiles. Only 3 have a firm or near-firm date. The blog put out 88 dated posts in April to September 2026 alone.
- **No steady rhythm that we can see.** The few dated items come in one-off bursts around launches and events: a wire release (about February 2025), a Product Hunt launch and a podcast (early 2026), and one founder LinkedIn post (31 July 2026).
- **The big news stayed on the blog.** We found no press release for the seed round or for the 14 September 2026 price change. Of six 2026 trade shows, only one left an outside trace we could find (a Product Hunt comment).
- **Old names and the old product are still live elsewhere.** Podcast notes say "CEO of Quotr.io" and "$5 million". An F6S listing and a YouTube tutorial still describe the old Revit add-in. quotr.io pages are still found in web search.
- **Credit where it is due.** The team did real work beyond the blog: six trade shows, a founder podcast, a Product Hunt launch, a webinar and a guest article. The gap is turning that work into pages on other sites that AI engines can cite.
- **Big caveat.** Quotr's LinkedIn company page, X, Instagram and Facebook output was never measured. "Thin" means "thin in what we could find", not "nothing was posted".

> [!note] Words used on this page
> - **Off-blog:** anything outside quotr.ai/blog, published by Quotr or about Quotr.
> - **Newswire:** a paid service that sends a press release to many news sites at once. The re-posted copies are called **syndicated** copies.
> - **Sitemap:** the list of pages a site gives to search engines. Its **lastmod** date (the "last changed" date) stands in for the publish date here.
> - **Handle:** an account name on a platform, such as @QuotrAI.
> - **Found in web search:** seen with our web search tool. The tool is not Google, so this does not prove a page is in Google.
> - **"How sure" labels:** **Read** = the vault opened the page on 2026-09-25. **Search only** = seen in search results or AI answers; the page was not opened. **Approx.** = the date was worked out, not shown on the page. **Sitemap** = sitemap date only.

## What this means

### Why thin off-blog publishing holds back AI visibility

- **AI engines lean on other people's sites.** In the Perplexity tests of 2026-09-25, we asked 32 unbranded prompts (questions that do not name Quotr). The top three sources were review sites: capterra.com and g2.com (in 9 prompts each) and sourceforge.net (in 8). r/estimators was a source in 6 ([[AI visibility baseline]]).
- **Industry data points the same way.** In Aleyda Solis's study of 2 August 2026, third-party sources made up 82.3% of citations for SaaS (software sold as an online subscription) in Google's AI Mode, Gemini and ChatGPT. YouTube was the largest SaaS source site, and Reddit appeared in all 15 SaaS panels (AI-20; carried over from the 2026-09-25 research, not re-searched on 2026-09-26).
- **AI finds Quotr's blog, but rarely names Quotr.** quotr.ai was in the sources for 6 of 32 unbranded prompts. Quotr was named in only 1 (3.1%). Its share of voice (its share of all brand mentions) was about 0.7%.
- **Quotr's facts mostly come from Quotr.** When a fact came only from quotr.ai, Perplexity often hedged with phrases like "vendor assertions" and "not independently validated" ([[Off-site presence]]).
- **What engines find outside quotr.ai is thin, and much of it is old.** The podcast says "$5 million" and "Quotr.io". Two vendor lists show the old price. F6S and a YouTube video describe the Revit add-in. Product Hunt files Quotr under "Real estate".
- **Big moments did not create outside sources we could find.** A seed round, a price change and six trade shows could each have produced a release, a LinkedIn post or a short video. We found one trace (the Dallas comment on Product Hunt). LinkedIn and YouTube histories were not checked, so there may be more.
- **The schema may point engines to an empty profile.** Medium is listed as an official profile, but no Quotr story turned up (not confirmed).
- **As the blog slows, nothing else we can see has picked up.** Blog output fell from 25 posts in June to 7 in September (to the 24th). The only dated item Quotr put out beyond the blog since June is one LinkedIn post. Social output was not measured, so this may understate what the team did.

This is a big part of [[What went wrong#W-02 Almost no third-party proof|W-02 Almost no third-party proof]]. The old quotr.io and test hosts are covered in [[What went wrong#W-17 Test-site and old-domain copies still in search indexes|W-17]].

### Fair context

- Quotr is a seed-stage company, about two years old. STACK, PlanSwift and Buildxact have had a decade or more to collect reviews and press ([[What went wrong]]).
- The team did real work beyond the blog: six trade shows in 2026, a founder podcast, a Product Hunt launch, a webinar and a guest article.
- On the podcast, Hanyang Liu already talked about GEO (getting named in AI answers) and unbranded search terms. That is exactly the right aim.
- Some gaps may have reasons we cannot see. The funding figure is not settled ($3.5M on quotr.ai, $5M on the podcast page), which makes a seed release harder to write ([[Q-06 Amounts and dates|Q-06]]).
- The 2026 Product Hunt page already uses the new quotr_ai and quotrai handles. Only its GitHub link keeps the old name.

### A simple rule for the year

- Every launch, event, dataset or price change gets at least one outside version: a release or pitch, a founder LinkedIn post, and a short video where it fits.
- Young rivals already work this way: Bobyard issued wire releases for its June and July 2026 trade launches ([[What went wrong]], [[Competitor publishing benchmark]]).
- The tasks that start this: [[B1 Seed-round announcement|B1]], [[B6 YouTube - one channel, and the series starts|B6]], [[B7 Founder presence - LinkedIn and estimator communities|B7]], [[C2 Press pitches built on the dataset|C2]]. Clean-up comes first: [[A13 Directory and profile clean-up|A13]] and [[A10 De-index the staging site; tidy legacy hosts and broken links|A10]].
- Track it monthly with KPIs L7 (reviews), L8 (list inclusions) and L10 (off-site mentions) in [[KPIs and dashboard]].
