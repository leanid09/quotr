# Template: Trade How-To Guide

> [!abstract] What this page is for
> A template for step-by-step trade guides ("how to do a drywall takeoff from PDF plans", "how to estimate plumbing from drawings") that AI engines can quote as steps, and that credit Quotr.ai by name where Quotr's method or data is used.

> [!info]- Sources
> [[competitor_geo_benchmark]] (§3 cited how-to pages and answer structure), [[quotr_ai_visibility_tests]] (§3 how-to prompts P1–P8), [[quotr_onsite_content_audit]] (§2 trade how-to cluster, §6 trade coverage), [[geo_content_playbook_b2b]] (§1, §2 click resilience, §7), [[verification_geo_evidence]] (claims #1, #2; H19); [[Prompt library]] (Stage 1.3 trade questions, including test N2).

---

## 1. Purpose and what the evidence says

- **Purpose:** win citations (and ideally a brand mention) on "how do I…" questions from estimators and trade owners, and lead readers to Quotr's trade page, a free resource or a trial.
- **What gets cited for how-to prompts today:** Bluebeam's dated "Construction Takeoffs Guide 2026", Buildxact's how-to posts, BuildVision AI's docs guide, Houzz Pro, Autodesk and ruh.ai glossary pages (competitor benchmark §3). The cited answer followed a clear structure: **numbered steps with units (each, LF, SF, CY), a "manual vs AI" rule of thumb, and a "still needs human checking" caveat.**
- **Quotr's pattern:** in test N2 ("how to estimate plumbing from drawings"), Quotr's plumbing post was the **first citation** and supplied most of the steps, but the answer never said "Quotr" (prompt library, Stage 1). Quotr's pages were **not** cited for "how to do a quantity takeoff from PDF plans / what is AI takeoff", even though they exist (competitor benchmark §3).
- **Click risk:** AI summarises generic how-tos well, so readers may not click. Aleyda Solis's "click resilience" test says to favour pages people still need to visit (to use a tool, see evidence, or act) (`geo_content_playbook_b2b.md` §2, §7). So every Quotr how-to should carry something AI cannot fully summarise: a worked example on a real plan set, a downloadable sheet, a calculator or a short video.
- **Don't fragment:** one strong guide per task, not one page per sub-question (`verification_geo_evidence.md` claim #2, H19).

## 3. Required sections, in order

| # | Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **H1** | "How to [task] from [plans/drawings] ([residential/commercial])" in the buyer's words |
| 2 | **Byline, dates** | Author with trade credential (e.g., "Estimator, 10 years in drywall") and/or "Reviewed by [estimator name, role]"; Published / Last updated |
| 3 | **Quick answer** | The method in 5–7 short numbered steps (40–90 words), with units |
| 4 | **What you need** | Drawings (which sheets), scale, units, tools (manual or software), typical time for a stated project size (sourced) |
| 5 | **Step-by-step** | One H3 per step: what to measure, the unit, the formula, a tip, a common mistake. Each step starts with a verb |
| 6 | **Worked example** | A real (or clearly labelled sample) plan: square footage, quantities per step, result table. Screenshots from Quotr.ai with alt text |
| 7 | **Manual vs AI takeoff** | Short table: time, what AI does well, what still needs a human check. **Brand-attributed** sentence for how Quotr.ai does it |
| 8 | **Quality check** | 5–8 checks before pricing (scale, openings, waste, missing sheets, addenda) |
| 9 | **Common mistakes** | 4–6 bullets, trade-specific |
| 10 | **Free resource** | Downloadable checklist or takeoff sheet, a calculator, or a 2–5 minute video (see [[YouTube video brief template]]) |
| 11 | **FAQ** | 4–6 questions from the prompt library |
| 12 | **Related terms and pages** | Dictionary terms, the trade page, pricing |
| 13 | **Sources** | Codes, manufacturer data sheets, associations, cost sources |

**Writing rules specific to how-tos:**
- Use the units estimators use: EA (each), LF, SF, CY, sheets, squares. Define each once.
- Put the number and unit in the step's first sentence.
- Every waste factor, production rate or cost is sourced (manufacturer, association, cost database) or labelled "Quotr data" with a method. Do not invent "typical" values.
- Name Quotr.ai in the manual-vs-AI section and the worked example, not in every step.

## 4. Filled-in example outline: "How to do a drywall takeoff from PDF plans (residential)"

Targets L-047, L-048, L-051. Numbers in brackets must be sourced before publishing.

**H1:** How to Do a Drywall Takeoff from PDF Plans for a House

**Byline:** By [Estimator name, role]. Reviewed by [name]. Published [date]. Last updated [date].

**Quick answer (draft):**
> 1. Set the scale on each floor plan. 2. Measure the linear feet (LF) of every wall that gets drywall, by wall type and height. 3. Multiply by wall height to get square feet (SF); add ceiling SF. 4. Subtract large openings per your deduction rule. 5. Divide by the sheet size (a 4x8 sheet covers 32 SF; a 4x12 sheet covers 48 SF) and add a waste factor of [X]% ([source]). 6. Add screws, tape, mud and corner bead from the SF total.

**Step H3s:**
1. Gather the right sheets (floor plans, reflected ceiling plans, sections, finish schedule) and check for addenda.
2. Set and verify the scale (use a known dimension).
3. Measure wall lengths by wall type (standard, moisture-resistant, fire-rated) in LF.
4. Convert to SF with wall heights; add ceilings and soffits.
5. Deduct openings (state the rule you use).
6. Convert SF to sheets by size; add waste ([source]).
7. Take off accessories (corner bead in LF; fasteners, tape and compound by SF using manufacturer coverage rates [source]).
8. Summarise by room and level for pricing.

**Worked example:** a [2,000 SF] single-family plan set from Quotr's sample deliverables ("All Trade Takeoff (Residential)" is listed in the /service/ sample library; **TO CONFIRM with Quotr** that it can be shown). Table: wall LF by type, wall SF, ceiling SF, deductions, sheets by size, waste, final count.

**Manual vs AI (draft, brand-attributed):**
> In Quotr.ai, you upload the PDF plan set; the AI measures wall lengths and calculates areas across every sheet, and each quantity stays editable, so the estimator checks low-confidence items before pricing. [Add time figures only from a sourced test or a named customer.]

**Common mistakes:** wrong scale on one sheet; missing ceiling areas; mixing wall types; forgetting garage fire-rated walls; no waste factor; ignoring addenda.

**Free resource:** drywall takeoff sheet (spreadsheet) or a 3-minute video of the same example in Quotr.ai.

**FAQ (from the prompt library):** "How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 2,000 sq ft house?" (answer with the method and a range only if sourced); "Should I use 4x8 or 4x12 sheets?"; "How much waste should I add?"; "Can AI do a drywall takeoff from a scanned PDF?"

**Links:** [/software/trades/drywall/](https://quotr.ai/software/trades/drywall/), dictionary terms (quantity-takeoff, ai-takeoff), [/pricing/](https://quotr.ai/pricing/), the commercial drywall + framing guide.

## 5. Quick fix for guides that are already cited (plumbing, electrical, HVAC)

These posts are already retrieved; they need the brand in the key sentences, not a rewrite:

1. Add a Quick answer block with the steps (if missing).
2. In the "manual vs AI" or tool section, add one brand-attributed sentence ("In Quotr.ai, …").
3. Add a worked example table with Quotr-specific numbers (sourced).
4. Replace "By quotr.ai" with a named author or reviewer.
5. Check dates, schema and internal links ([[Page refresh checklist]]).
